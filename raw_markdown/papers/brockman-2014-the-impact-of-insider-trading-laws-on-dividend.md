## Abstract (English)

We posit that firms use dividend payout policy to reduce information asymmetry and agency costs caused by country-level institutional weaknesses. Firms operating in countries with weak insider trading laws attempt to mitigate this institutional weakness by committing themselves to paying out large and stable cash dividends. We test this central hypothesis (among others) using an international sample of firms across 24 countries, as well as by conducting a case study during an enforcement action. The results show that weak insider trading laws lead to a higher propensity of paying dividends, larger dividend amounts and greater dividend smoothing. We also show that the market's valuation of dividend payouts is significantly higher when insider trading protection is weak. It is important to note that these insider trading results are not due to cross-country variations in investor or creditor protection, nor are they contingent on the enforcement of insider trading laws. Overall, our evidence supports the view that dividend payouts serve as a substitute bonding mechanism when country-level legal protections fail. (C) 2014 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.

## 摘要（中文翻译）

我们提出，企业利用股利支付政策来降低由国家层面制度薄弱所导致的信息不对称和代理成本。在内幕交易法薄弱的国家经营的企业，试图通过承诺支付大额且稳定的现金股利来缓解这一制度缺陷。我们使用涵盖24个国家的国际企业样本，并结合一次执法行动期间的案例研究，检验了这一核心假设（及其他假设）。结果表明，薄弱的内幕交易法导致更高的股利支付倾向、更大的股利金额以及更强的股利平滑。我们还表明，当内幕交易保护薄弱时，市场对股利支付的估值显著更高。值得注意的是，这些内幕交易结果并非源于投资者保护或债权人保护的跨国差异，也不取决于内幕交易法的执行。总体而言，我们的证据支持这样一种观点：当国家层面的法律保护失效时，股利支付充当了一种替代性的约束机制。
