# Unintended Benefits of Mandatory Dividend Regulations on Investment Efficiency: Evidence from China

- **Authors:** Chan, Raymond Siu Yeung; Fan, Lyu; Song, Byron Y.
- **Journal:** Accounting and Finance
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1111/acfi.12829

## Abstract

We investigate the unintended benefits of mandatory dividend payout regulations for Chinese listed firms seeking refinancing to enhance their investment efficiency, even though the initial intention of these regulations is to protect powerless external minority shareholders. We find that, while these regulations mitigate the overinvestment of all listed firms, this effect is more pronounced among refinancing firms than among non-refinancing firms. We further find evidence partially showing that the mitigating effect of these regulations on overinvestment is driven mainly by state-owned enterprises (SOEs). These findings suggest dividend payout regulations are used to partially ameliorate Chinese listed firms' investment inefficiency, particularly those of SOEs.

Key words: Dividend payout; State-owned enterprises; Overinvestment; Investment efficiency; China

### Regulatory Background

China's CSRC issued a series of mandatory dividend payout regulations:
- 2004: Prohibition on firms that did not pay dividends in the prior three years from conducting seasoned equity or convertible bond offerings (refinancing).
- 2006: Minimum cumulative payout ratio of 20% of net profit in the prior three years for refinancing firms.
- 2008: Minimum payout ratio increased to 30%; payouts must be in cash rather than shares.

These regulations link public refinancing eligibility to dividend distribution.

### Sample

7,274 firm-year observations of Chinese listed firms, fiscal years 2001–2011. 3,018 observations classified as overinvestments.

### Hypotheses

- H1: The mitigating effect of cash dividends on overinvestment is stronger after the 2006 regulation (especially for refinancing firms).
- H2a/H2b: Effect on underinvestment is ambiguous.
- H3: The regulation's overinvestment-mitigating effect is stronger for SOEs than for non-SOEs.

### Main Results

- Since 2006, cash dividend distributions mitigate overinvestment for all listed firms, on average; the effect is more pronounced for refinancing firms than non-refinancing firms (H1 confirmed).
- No significant effect of the regulation on underinvestment (H2a supported).
- The overinvestment-mitigating effect is driven mainly by SOEs; the impact on non-SOEs is only minor (H3 confirmed).
- Similar results for the 2008 regulation.

### Identification

- Regression models use lagged dividend payouts to account for timing.
- Propensity score matching (PSM) + difference-in-differences analysis uses first-visit year (regulation implementation year) as event; tests interaction effects to mitigate reverse causality.

### Interpretation

Although the 2006 and 2008 regulations were designed to protect minority shareholders from expropriation, they produced an unintended benefit: constraining free cash flows reduced SOEs' propensity to overinvest. SOEs face soft budget constraints and lack performance incentives; dividend mandates reduced their available cash and limited empire-building. The effect is weaker for non-SOEs because they are already subject to harder budget constraints and invest more efficiently.

### Robustness

Results are qualitatively similar under an alternative investment efficiency model and an alternative dividend payout regulation specification.
