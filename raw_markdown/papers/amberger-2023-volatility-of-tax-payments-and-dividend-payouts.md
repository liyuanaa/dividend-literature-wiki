# Volatility of Tax Payments and Dividend Payouts

- **Authors:** Amberger, Harald J.
- **Journal:** Contemporary Accounting Research
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** 10.1111/1911-3846.12831

## Abstract

Dividends are a key mechanism for shareholders to discipline managers and mitigate agency conflicts. This study examines whether the volatility of tax payments is associated with dividend payouts. Consistent with the predictions, results suggest that firms with more volatile tax payments are less likely to pay dividends overall and their dividends are lower in magnitude when doing so. These effects are economically significant and incremental to a firm's operating risk. The link between volatile tax payments and the likelihood of dividend payouts is weaker for firms that distribute dividends to alleviate agency conflicts. Similarly, the link between volatile tax payments and the amount of dividend payouts is weaker for firms that hold more cash for tax reasons. Taken together, these findings add to our understanding of the economic consequences of volatile tax payments and the determinants of dividend payouts.

Keywords: volatility of tax payments, cash ETR volatility, dividends, tax uncertainty, tax risk, real effects

Sample: US public firms, fiscal years 1993–2014 (effective coverage 1998–2014). Full sample: 33,023 firm-year observations (4,569 unique firms); subsample of dividend payers: 13,694 observations (1,843 firms). Financial firms (SIC 6000–6999) and utilities (SIC 4900–4949) excluded.

### Measure

CVTax = coefficient of variation of annual cash effective tax rates (ETRs) over the prior five years (McGuire et al. 2013), orthogonalized by industry-year with respect to proxies for operating risk (CVCashFlow, CVRoa, CVSales) and losses (LossFirm), isolating the tax-strategy component of tax payment volatility.

### Main Results

- A one standard deviation increase in CVTax is associated with a 4.9 percentage point lower probability of dividend payouts (logit regression, H1 confirmed).
- Among dividend-paying firms, a one standard deviation increase in CVTax is associated with 3% lower dividend levels, equivalent to $6.8 million less for the average dividend payer (H2 confirmed).
- The effect of volatile tax payments on payouts is incremental to operating risk.

### Cross-Sectional Tests

- Agency motives moderate the effect: firms with high institutional ownership (top annual quartile) show no significant relation between CVTax and dividend probability; firms with low institutional ownership show a significant negative relation (p<0.01).
- Firms with high unrecognized tax benefit (UTB) balances show a weaker (insignificant) relation between CVTax and dividend levels, consistent with precautionary cash holdings for tax reasons buffering the effect.
- Firms with higher borrowing costs (unrated) show a stronger negative relation between CVTax and dividend probability.
- Growing firms (low RETE) show weaker relation between CVTax and dividend levels (lower costs of dividend reductions).

### Additional Findings

- Current CVTax is informative about future dividend payouts.
- Firms with more volatile tax payments prefer more flexible distribution channels such as share repurchases or special dividends.
- Results are robust to alternative measures: standard deviation of cash ETRs (SDTax), large-tax-payment proxy (HighTaxVol), GAAP-based CVTax (GAAPCVTax), and changes specification.
