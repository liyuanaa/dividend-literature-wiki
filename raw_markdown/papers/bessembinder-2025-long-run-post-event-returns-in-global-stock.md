## Abstract (English)

Firms often experience significant abnormal stock returns following major corporate events, but these returns vary widely across countries. We examine how national institutions-particularly financial system development, investor protection, and governance practices-influence post-event stock returns for over 164,000 events across 58 countries. Our analysis covers six frequent corporate events: dividend initiations, share repurchases, seasoned equity offerings, stock splits, mergers and acquisitions, and initial public offerings. Using a characteristic-based benchmarking approach that accounts for cross-country differences in firm fundamentals, we find that firms in countries with more developed financial systems and stronger investor protections exhibit significantly smaller long-run post-event abnormal returns. These findings are robust to alternative benchmarks and model specifications. Our results suggest that the institutional environment can reduce informational frictions and promote more efficient investor responses to corporate announcements. In countries with strong financial and governance institutions, market reactions to corporate events are more muted-indicating fewer pricing anomalies and more efficient capital markets. By demonstrating how national institutions help shape the outcomes of firm-level events, our study highlights the value of incorporating institutional context into international finance research and helps explain why post-event return patterns often differ across countries. ## 摘要（中文翻译）

企业在重大公司事件发生后往往会经历显著的股票异常收益，但这些收益在不同国家之间差异很大。我们考察了国家层面的制度——尤其是金融体系发展程度、投资者保护和治理实践——如何影响58个国家超过164,000起事件后的股票收益。我们的分析涵盖六类常见的公司事件：股利首次派发、股份回购、增发股票（seasoned equity offerings）、股票拆分、并购以及首次公开发行。采用一种基于特征的基准化方法（该方法考虑了各国企业基本面的差异），我们发现，位于金融体系更发达、投资者保护更强的国家的企业，其长期事件后异常收益显著更小。这些发现对于替代基准和不同的模型设定都是稳健的。我们的结果表明，制度环境可以降低信息摩擦，并促使投资者对公司公告作出更有效率的反应。在拥有强健金融与治理制度的国家，市场对公司事件的反应更为温和——这表明定价异常更少、资本市场更有效率。通过展示国家制度如何帮助塑造企业层面事件的结果，我们的研究凸显了将制度背景纳入国际金融研究的价值，并有助于解释为何事件后收益模式在不同国家之间常常存在差异。
