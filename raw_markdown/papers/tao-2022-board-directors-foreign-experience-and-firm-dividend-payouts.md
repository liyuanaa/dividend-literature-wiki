# Board Directors' Foreign Experience and Firm Dividend Payouts

- **Authors:** Tao, Qizhi; Wei, K.C. John; Xiang, Xueman; Yi, Biao
- **Journal:** Journal of Corporate Finance
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jcorpfin.2022.102237

## Abstract

We find that Chinese-listed firms are more likely to pay dividends and implement larger payout ratios when their directors have foreign experience. This finding remains after using various methods to address potential endogeneity concerns. The positive association is stronger for low-growth firms, non-state-owned firms, and when directors obtain foreign experience in countries with stronger investor protection. The effect of directors' foreign experience on dividend payouts is also more pronounced for independent directors than for executive directors. Directors with foreign experience appear to overcome the weak investor protection environment as the effects are more pronounced among poorly governed firms and before the regulatory dividend reforms in 2013. Overall, our findings identify a mechanism through which better governance practices are propagated.

### 1. Introduction

The board of directors is critical for corporate strategy and firm decisions. Recent literature shows that directors' foreign experience is associated with better firm performance (Giannetti et al., 2015) and corporate governance (Iliev and Roth, 2018). China has experienced rapid economic development but growth remains hampered by weak legal institutions, weak investor protection, and underdeveloped labor markets.

The paper investigates whether directors in China with foreign experience transfer advanced corporate governance practices by examining dividend payout policies. A director is considered to have foreign experience if he or she has studied or worked outside mainland China. The study focuses on agency conflict as the main mechanism, given that dividend payments can serve as effective corporate governance devices for mitigating agency problems (Rozeff, 1982; La Porta et al., 2000; DeAngelo et al., 2006).

China has no capital gains tax and the ordinary income tax rate is 0% for stocks held for more than a year, so the analysis focuses on agency conflicts rather than tax considerations. Prior studies show that dividend payouts for Chinese listed firms produce no signaling effect (Lv and Xu, 2010; Xiao, 2010).

### 2. Background and Hypotheses

**Chinese dividend regulations timeline:**
- 2001: CSRC requires lead underwriter to explain dividend situation.
- 2004: First regulation tying dividend payments to SEO/convertible bond eligibility.
- 2006: Cumulative payout ratio ≥ 20% required for qualified issuers.
- 2008: Minimum cumulative payout ratio raised to 30%.
- January 2013 (SSE): World's first comply-or-explain dividend regulation — 30% minimum payout recommended; non-compliance requires explanation.
- November 2013 (CSRC): Guidelines No. 3 — differential policy with payout ratios 20%–80% based on development stage; non-compliance recorded in integrity files.

**Hypotheses:**
- H1: Directors with foreign experience increase the likelihood of dividend payment and payout ratio.
- H2: Good corporate governance moderates the effect (substitution effect among governance mechanisms).
- H3: Effect is stronger when foreign experience is gained in countries with stronger investor protection.
- H4: Effect is stronger before the 2013 dividend regulations (external regulation substitutes for governance).

### 3. Data and Research Design

**Sample:** All A-share companies on SSE and SZSE, 2008–2018. Excludes financial firms, ST/*ST firms, and observations with missing data. Final: 3,480 firms, 25,005 firm-year observations. Data from CSMAR.

**Main explanatory variable:** Directors FE = proportion of directors with foreign experience (study or work outside mainland China). Between 2008 and 2018, the percentage of firms with at least one FE director rose from 40.3% to 52.3%. Mean proportion of FE directors on board: ~11.4%–22.8% annually.

**Dependent variables:** Payer (dummy = 1 if firm pays dividends); Dividends/NI. Robustness: Dividends/Sales, Dividends/TA, Dividends/CF, DPS.

**Baseline model:** Logit (for Payer) or OLS (for payout ratio) with industry and year fixed effects, standard errors clustered at firm level.

**Control variables:** Size, Leverage, OCF, ROA, Retained Earnings, Growth, M/B, Volatility, SOE, Board Size, Female, Ind Directors, Foreign Share.

### 4. Main Results

**Dividend payer likelihood (Table 4, Panel A):**
- Directors FE coefficient = 0.875 (z = 4.67***) with full controls.
- Economic significance: one SD increase in Directors FE → 2.17 pp increase in dividend likelihood.
- Independent directors (Ind Directors FE): coefficient = 1.188 (z = 4.34***) → 1.93 pp increase.
- Executive directors (Exe Directors FE): coefficient = 0.559 (z = 1.88*) → 0.92 pp increase.
- Both FE Education (0.953***) and FE Work (0.909***) subcategories are significant.

**Payout ratio (Table 4, Panel B):**
- Directors FE coefficient = 0.072 (t = 3.95***).
- Ind Directors FE = 0.090 (t = 3.39***); Exe Directors FE = 0.053 (t = 1.83*).
- FE Education = 0.069 (t = 2.77***); FE Work = 0.087 (t = 2.79***).

**Endogeneity tests:**
- IV approach (Table 5): Instruments = (1) number of Christian missionary colleges in province established before 1920; (2) proportion of non-FE directors linked to FE directors on other boards. Both instruments are positive and significant predictors of Directors FE (F-stat > 22). Second-stage results confirm positive and significant effect of Directors FE on all payout measures.
- Heckman two-step (Table 6): After controlling for selection bias (IMR), Directors FE remains positive and significant.
- Firm fixed-effects model: results hold (Table OA4).

### 5. Cross-Sectional Mechanisms (Tables 7–10)

**Governance substitution (Table 7):**
- Effect significantly stronger for firms with weak corporate governance (low governance index score, no blockholder).
- Supports H2: FE directors substitute for absent governance mechanisms.

**Country quality of foreign experience (Table 8):**
- Effect significantly stronger when foreign experience is in countries with higher La Porta et al. investor protection index.
- Supports H3.

**SOE vs. non-SOE (Table 9):**
- Effect significantly stronger for non-SOE firms.
- SOE dividend policy constrained by government; FE directors have less discretion.

**Firm growth (Table 9):**
- Effect significantly stronger for low-growth (mature) firms with high retained earnings.
- Consistent with optimal dividend hypothesis: mature firms should optimally distribute more.

### 6. Before vs. After 2013 Regulations (Table 10)

- Effect of Directors FE on dividend payouts is significantly more pronounced before the 2013 SSE comply-or-explain regulation.
- After 2013, external dividend regulations partially substitute for the governance role of FE directors.
- Supports H4.

### 7. Dividend Flexibility (Table 11)

Directors' foreign experience has no significant effect on dividend downward flexibility (propensity to cut dividends). Chinese firms' dividends are already downwardly flexible (comparable to German firms), and FE directors do not further alter this flexibility.

### 8. Conclusion

Directors with foreign experience increase dividend payouts in Chinese listed firms, primarily through independent directors using dividends as a monitoring tool to reduce agency costs of free cash flows. The effect is strongest for poorly governed, non-SOE, mature firms and in countries with strong investor protection environments. The effect diminishes after mandatory dividend regulations in 2013, demonstrating the substitution between internal governance and external regulatory requirements. The findings identify a mechanism through which governance practices diffuse internationally.
