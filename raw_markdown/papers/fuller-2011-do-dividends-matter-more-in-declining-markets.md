## Abstract (English)

We find dividends do matter to shareholders, but more in declining markets than advancing ones. Dividend-paying stocks outperform non-dividend-paying stocks by 1 to 2% more per month in declining markets than in advancing markets. These results are economically and statistically significant and robust to many risk adjustments and across industries. In addition, we find an asymmetric response to dividend changes based on market conditions: dividend increases matter more in declining markets than advancing ones. Tests indicate that results are not due to more profitable firms and appear not to be caused either by free cash flow or signaling explanations. We also find that it is the existence of dividends, and not the dividend yield, that drives returns' asymmetric behavior relative to market movements. (C) 2011 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.

## 摘要（中文翻译）

我们发现股利对股东确实重要，但在下跌市场中比在上涨市场中更为重要。在下跌市场中，派息股票每月跑赢不派息股票的幅度比在上涨市场中多1至2个百分点。这些结果在经济上和统计上都显著，并且对许多风险调整以及跨行业都稳健。此外，我们发现基于市场状况对股利变化存在不对称的反应：股利增长在下跌市场中比在上涨市场中更为重要。检验表明结果并非源于更盈利的企业，且似乎既不是由自由现金流也不是由信号传递解释所引起的。我们还发现，是股利的存在与否、而非股利收益率，驱动了收益相对于市场变动的不对称行为。
