# Active catering to dividend clienteles: Evidence from takeovers

- **Authors:** Golubov, Andrey; Lasfer, Meziane; Vitkova, Valeriya
- **Journal:** Journal of Financial Economics
- **Year:** 2020
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.04.002

## Abstract

We use merger-induced changes to shareholder structure to test for active catering to dividend clienteles. Following mergers, acquirers adjust their dividend payout toward that of the target, but only when they inherit target shareholders through stock swaps. This adjustment is stronger when legacy shareholders are more influential and reveal a greater preference for dividends through portfolio holdings and trading behavior. Country-level differences in dividend taxes, governance quality, and population age further shape the extent of adjustment in ways consistent with dividend preferences. Pre-closing, differences in dividend payout discourage the use of stock as a payment method.

## 1. Introduction

Why do firms pay dividends and what could explain payout differences across firms? According to the dividend clientele theory, investor clienteles with varying characteristics have different levels of preference for dividends. Some investors favor high-dividend-paying firms due to differential tax status, while others view dividends and capital gains as disconnected attributes due to mental accounting. The authors use merger-induced changes to shareholder structure to test whether companies actively cater to dividend preferences of their investors.

In a stock-swap merger, the acquirer inherits the shareholders of the target. If the pre-merger dividend policies of the two firms are different, the newly acquired dividend clientele could compel the acquirer to adjust its dividend payout toward that of the target. Cash-based acquisitions provide a control group, as cash deals do not entail a change in the shareholder base.

## 3. Sample and Methodology

Global sample of 5,366 public-to-public M&A transactions of listed firms, 1990–2015, from Thomson Reuters SDC. Acquirers from OECD member countries. Excluded: financial (SIC 6000–6999) and utility (SIC 4900–4949) firms, and deals without control transfer. Financial data from Thomson Reuters Datastream. Main outcome variable: change in acquirer DPS (ΔDPSj = (DPS_Acq,+j − DPS_Acq,−1) / BPS_Acq,−1) over 1-, 2-, and 3-year post-merger windows.

Dividend Gap = Dividend Yield_Tar − Dividend Yield_Acq (positive = target preferred higher dividends). Main test: interaction term Dividend Gap × Pct. Stock captures the clientele shift.

## 4. Baseline Results

**Univariate (Tables 2–3):** When Dividend Gap > 0, acquirers' DPS increases by 14.22% of pre-merger BPS in year 1, vs. 5.76% when Dividend Gap ≤ 0 (difference = 8.46pp, p < 0.01). The difference in adjustment magnitude is concentrated in stock-swap deals (Panel B: 14.23pp) vs. cash deals (Panel C: 4.11pp); the difference-in-differences is 10.12pp (p < 0.01).

**Multivariate (Table 4):** Dividend Gap alone has no significant effect; Dividend Gap × Pct. Stock is positive and highly significant (0.2757 for year 1, 0.3871 for year 2, 0.4531 for year 3; all p < 0.01). This interaction term captures the clientele change. In pure cash deals, differences in dividend policy do not affect combined firm payout. Pure stock-for-stock subsample confirms clientele interpretation.

## 5. Cross-Sectional Differences

**Firm- and deal-level (Table 5):** Extent of adjustment is greater when the target is larger relative to acquirer (Dividend Gap × Pct. Stock × Rel. Size, p < 0.01 in years 1–2), and when acquirer stock is more illiquid (Dividend Gap × Pct. Stock × Amihud Illiquidity, p < 0.01 across all years).

**Ownership characteristics — US targets (Table 6):** Adjustment stronger when target shareholders include more dedicated institutional investors (low-turnover, concentrated portfolios; triple interaction p < 0.05). Stronger when target shareholders reveal greater preference for dividends via portfolio holdings (Clientele Preference; p < 0.05). Stronger when target shareholders are less tax-sensitive (Tax-Insensitive Clientele; p < 0.05).

**Country-level (Table 7):** Adjustment weaker when acquirer comes from a country with better investor protection than the target (Δ Anti-self-dealing negative and significant, p < 0.05). Stronger when acquirer is from a country with full/partial dividend imputation tax regime (p < 0.01). Stronger when target is domiciled in a country with a higher proportion of senior citizens (p < 0.01).

## 6. Additional Tests

Pre-closing, the likelihood of an all-stock payment is strongly negatively associated with the difference between target and acquirer dividend yields — large dividend gaps discourage stock-based payment. Fraction of consideration in stock is also negatively associated with Dividend Gap. This demonstrates ex-ante effects of clientele considerations on deal structure.

## 7. Conclusion

Merger-induced changes to the shareholder base provide strong evidence that firms actively cater to dividend clienteles. The effect operates only through stock swaps (where target shareholders are inherited), is stronger for more influential and dividend-preferring clienteles, and is consistent with tax-based, agency-based, and age-based demand differences at the country level.
