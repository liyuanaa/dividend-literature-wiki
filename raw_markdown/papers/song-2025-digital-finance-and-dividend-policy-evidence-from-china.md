# Digital Finance and Dividend Policy: Evidence from China

- **Authors:** Song, Yunxing; Lee, Suin; Wang, Bin
- **Journal:** Pacific-Basin Finance Journal
- **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.pacfin.2025.102704

## Abstract

This study investigates the effect of digital finance on dividend payouts. Using the digital finance index from PKU-DFICC and a sample of Chinese listed firms, we find a positive relation between digital finance and dividend payouts. Supporting our hypothesis that digital finance reduces information asymmetry and increases insiders' incentive to pay out dividends, the positive relation is stronger for firms with severe information asymmetry. Additionally, digital finance's role in reducing financing constraints also promotes dividend payouts. Overall, our findings suggest noteworthy implications of digital finance on financial markets via dividend payout policy.

### 1. Introduction

Digital finance blends new financial services with traditional ones via internet and information technology, significantly transforming capital market operations. The paper examines how the development of digital finance influences corporate dividend payouts through two primary channels:

1. **Information asymmetry channel:** Digital finance reduces information asymmetry between insiders and outsiders, thereby reducing insiders' incentive to hoard cash. More transparent environments raise the cost of unethical behaviors, resulting in higher dividend payouts.

2. **Financing constraints channel:** Digital finance alleviates enterprise financing constraints and improves financial resilience, enabling firms to maintain or increase dividend payouts with greater confidence.

China provides an excellent research setting: (a) it is at the forefront of digital finance development; (b) imperfect market construction and prevalent information asymmetry; (c) Chinese firms with highly concentrated corporate ownership tend to underpay dividends to shareholders (Allen et al., 2005; Jiang et al., 2017).

### 2. Data

**Digital finance measure (primary):** PKU-DFICC (Peking University Digital Financial Inclusion Index of China), based on Ant Group data. Three dimensions:
- DF_Breadth: extent of digital finance service coverage.
- DF_Depth: utilization depth of digital financial services (payment, insurance, credit, investment).
- DF_Digital: inclusiveness/convenience of digital finance services.

Provincial-level digital finance index used as primary measure; city-level used for robustness.

**Sample:** Chinese listed companies, 2011–2021 (CSMAR database). Excludes financial firms and ST firms. Final sample: 18,381 firm-year observations for 2,507 non-financial firms.

**Dividend measures (following Jiang et al., 2017):**
- DVE = cash dividends / earnings per share (dividend payout ratio)
- DVC = total cash dividends / cash flow from operating activities
- DVP = dummy (1 if total dividends > 0)

**Control variables:** Size, ROA, Q (Tobin's Q), Lev, Age, Cash, Top1, Independence, GDP_growth. Year and industry fixed effects. Robust standard errors clustered by firm.

**Descriptive statistics (Table 1):** Mean digital finance index = 5.479 (SD = 0.561). Mean DVP = 0.823 (82.3% of firms pay dividends). Mean DVE = 0.299, mean DVC = 0.163.

### 4. Empirical Results

**4.1 Baseline regressions (Table 2, Panel A):**
Digital finance is positively and significantly associated with all three dividend measures:
- DVE: coefficient = 0.079*** (se = 0.023). Economic significance: one SD increase → 14.8% increase in mean DVE.
- DVC: coefficient = 0.080*** (se = 0.026). One SD → 27.5% increase in mean DVC.
- DVP: coefficient = 1.158*** (se = 0.145).

**Panel B — Components:**
- DF_Breadth: significant for DVE (0.033**), DVC (0.038**), DVP (0.549***).
- DF_Depth: significant for DVE (0.091***), DVC (0.093***), DVP (1.029***).
- DF_Digital: not significant for any dividend measure — digitization of inclusive finance (targeting individuals/SMEs) does not materially affect corporate payout policies.

**4.2 Information asymmetry channel (Table 3):**
Subsample tests confirm the effect is stronger for firms with higher information asymmetry:
- **Analyst coverage (Panel A):** Low coverage: DVE = 0.172*** vs. high coverage: 0.013 (insignificant); difference significant at 0.1%.
- **ESG performance (Panel B):** Effect similar but difference in DVP significant at 0.1%.
- **SOE ownership (Panel C):** SOE: DVE = 0.004 (insignificant) vs. non-SOE: 0.090*** (significant); difference significant at 0.3%.
- **Firm size (Panel D):** Small firms: DVE = 0.106*** vs. large firms: 0.047 (insignificant); difference significant at 5%.
- **Managerial ownership (Panel E):** Management control: DVE = 0.069** (stronger effect) vs. no control: 0.049 (insignificant).

**4.3 Financing constraints channel (Table 4):**
- First stage: Digital finance significantly reduces KZ index (coefficient = −0.213*), confirming that digital finance alleviates financial constraints.
- Second stage: KZ index significantly negatively predicts all three dividend measures (DVE: −0.095***, DVC: −0.054***, DVP: −0.514***).
- Consistent with a mediation pathway: digital finance → reduces financing constraints → increases dividends.

**4.4 Identification:**
- **DID test (Table 5):** Exploiting the 2016 G20 Digital Inclusive Finance Senior Principles release as a shock particularly affecting central/western regions. Treat×Post coefficient: DVE = 0.096** (se = 0.010), DVC = 0.060** (se = 0.008), DVP = 0.517** (se = 0.258). Supports causal interpretation.
- **2SLS-IV (Table 6):** Instrument = mobile phone users per 100 people (MPU). First stage: MPU positively predicts digital finance (coefficient = 0.471***). Second stage: instrumented digital finance positively and significantly predicts DVE (0.131*), DVC (0.265**), DVP (0.983***).

**4.5 Robustness:**
- City-level digital finance index (Table 7): results hold (DVE = 0.074***, DVC = 0.123***, DVP = 1.770***).
- Tobit model regressions (Table 8): DVE = 0.123***, DVC = 0.144***.

### 5. Conclusion

Digital finance development in China positively and causally affects corporate dividend payouts. The effect operates through two channels: (1) reducing information asymmetry, which increases insiders' incentive to distribute cash to shareholders; and (2) alleviating financing constraints, enabling firms to commit to higher and more stable dividend payments. The effect is more pronounced for firms with greater information asymmetry (small size, low analyst coverage, non-SOE, managerial control). Digital finance breadth and depth drive the effect; the digitization-of-inclusiveness dimension does not. The findings highlight digital finance as an important external governance mechanism that shapes corporate payout policies.
