## Abstract (English)

We examine corporate payout policy in dual-class firms. The expropriation hypothesis predicts that dual-class firms pay out less to shareholders because entrenched managers want to maximize the value of assets under control and the associated private benefits. The pre-commitment hypothesis predicts that dual-class firms pay out more to shareholders because firms use corporate payouts as a pre-commitment device to mitigate agency costs. Our results support the pre-commitment hypothesis. Dual-class firms have higher cash dividend payments and total payouts, and they use more regular cash dividends rather than special dividends or repurchases, compared to their propensity-matched single-class firms. Dual-class firms with severe free cash flow-related agency problems and few growth opportunities rely even more on corporate payouts as a pre-commitment mechanism. We also rule out the alternative explanation that dual-class firms pay out more because super-voting shareholders lack the ability to generate home-made dividends by selling shares since super-voting shares are often non-tradable or very illiquid. ## 摘要（中文翻译）

我们考察了双重股权结构企业的公司支付政策。侵占假说预测，双重股权结构企业向股东支付较少，因为壕沟自守的管理者希望最大化其所控制资产的价值以及相关的私人收益。预先承诺假说则预测，双重股权结构企业向股东支付更多，因为企业将公司支付用作缓解代理成本的预先承诺工具。我们的结果支持预先承诺假说。与倾向得分匹配的单一股权结构企业相比，双重股权结构企业的现金股利支付和总支付更高，且更多地使用常规现金股利而非特别股利或回购。存在严重的自由现金流相关代理问题且成长机会较少的双重股权结构企业，更加依赖公司支付作为预先承诺机制。我们还排除了另一种解释，即双重股权结构企业支付更多是因为超级投票权股东缺乏通过出售股份来自制股利的能力，因为超级投票权股份往往不可交易或流动性极差。
