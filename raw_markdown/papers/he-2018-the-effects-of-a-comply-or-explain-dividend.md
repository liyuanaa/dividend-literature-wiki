## Abstract (English)

We examine the effects of the world's first comply-or-explain dividend regulation in China's Shanghai Stock Exchange, which requires firms to either pay at least 30% of profits as dividends or explain the use of funds. We find that many firms increased their payout ratio to comply, by increasing dividends or decreasing earnings. Firms with high profitability, state ownership, and fewer agency conflicts were more likely to comply. However, complying firms subsequently issued more debt and had a decline in accounting performance and firm valuation. The evidence China suggests that the comply-or-explain regulation increased firms' dividends at substantial costs.

## 摘要（中文翻译）

我们考察世界上首个"遵守或解释"股利监管在中国上海证券交易所所产生的影响，该监管要求企业要么将至少30%的利润作为股利支付，要么解释资金用途。我们发现，许多企业通过增加股利或减少收益来提高其支付率以求遵守。盈利能力高、国有所有权以及代理冲突较少的企业更可能遵守。然而，遵守的企业随后发行了更多债务，并出现会计业绩和企业估值的下降。来自中国的证据表明，"遵守或解释"监管以巨大的代价提高了企业的股利。
