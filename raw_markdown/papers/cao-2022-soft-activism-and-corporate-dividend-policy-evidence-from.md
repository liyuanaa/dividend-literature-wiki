# Soft Activism and Corporate Dividend Policy: Evidence from Institutional Investors' Site Visits

- **Authors:** Cao, Jerry; Wang, Hanyang; Zhou, Sili
- **Journal:** Journal of Corporate Finance
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jcorpfin.2022.102221

## Abstract (English)

We study the governance effect of institutional site visits and focus on corporate payout. Due to the weak investor protection and poor information quality in China, institutional investors resort to site visits as a form of soft activism. We show that site visits lead to an increase of corporate cash dividend, through the mechanism of disciplining management with exit threats. Such effect is more pronounced for visits by state-owned institutions. We further confirm the causal link with both difference-in-difference analysis and instrumental variable regression. The paper contributes to the research on soft activism and governance device of institutional investors in weak institutional environments.

## Full Text (PDF Conversion)

Published in: Journal of Corporate Finance, 75 (2022), 102221.

### Setting

China's SHSE and SZSE, established 1990/1991. The combined A-share market is the second largest in the world by market cap. Institutional investor presence grew rapidly after the 2008 financial crisis. Since 2009, SZSE mandated firms to disclose site visit information in annual reports; since July 2012, a standard summary report must be disclosed within two trading days of a visit. Sample covers 2009–2016.

### Site Visits as Soft Activism

Site visits: trips by institutional investors (asset management, funds, securities, banks, insurance, investment companies, private equity, trusts) to a firm's headquarters and production facilities. Visitors physically inspect operations, meet managers, ask detailed questions. Number of site visits increased from 9,429 (2009) to 66,799 (2016). Manufacturing accounts for ~64% of visited firms.

### Main Findings

- Firms with institutional site visits increase cash dividend payout ratio (DPS/EPS) by 1.4% in the subsequent year compared to firms without visits.
- A one-standard-deviation increase in site visit intensity leads to 4.1% increase in the standard deviation of the payout ratio.
- Effect confirmed causally via: (i) propensity score matching + difference-in-difference (first-visit year as event year); (ii) instrumental variable (distance between firm's and visitor's headquarter city).

### Mechanism: Exit Threat

- Visiting fund managers are significantly more likely to sell equity stakes in firms that underpay dividends relative to peers.
- Affiliated analysts (working in brokerages charging commissions to the visiting fund) also strengthen the exit threat: firms visited by affiliated analysts see decreased fund holding when they underpay dividends.
- Exit threat is the primary mechanism: soft activism alone (communication) is insufficient; the credible threat of exit is what disciplines managers.

### Heterogeneity

- Effect is similar in magnitude for SOEs and non-SOEs (unlike some prior studies).
- State-owned institutional investors have a stronger dividend-inducing effect than non-state-owned institutions (longer investment horizon, greater power).

### Identification

- PSM controls for observable pre-visit characteristics.
- IV (headquarter distance) addresses unobservable heterogeneity.
- Results are robust to alternative definitions of site visit intensity and to controlling for analyst-related information effects.
