# Tunneling and dividend payouts: A tale of agency conflicts in China

- **Authors:** Zeng, Sipeng; Qu, Zhi; Zhou, Kuo
- **Journal:** Journal of Accounting and Public Policy
- **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jaccpubpol.2025.107401

## Abstract

Related party transactions (RPTs) are often viewed as a means for majority shareholders to tunnel, while dividends ensure fair payout to both majority and minority shareholders. Using a sample of Chinese listed firms from 2002 to 2022, our study documents a negative relationship between RPT-based tunneling and the dividend payout ratio, a finding that is contrary to the existing literature on the relationship between the tunneling effect and dividend payout ratios in the U.S. market. In addition, our study finds that external monitoring, as opposed to internal governance, significantly limits the ability of large shareholders to reduce dividend payouts through tunneling. Further mechanism analysis shows that RPT tunneling reduces dividend payments by reducing available free cash flow. Minority shareholders' insufficient awareness of the damage of controlling shareholders' tunneling through related party transactions is an important reason for the existence of this negative correlation in China. This paper, in conjunction with the prior literature, suggests that the relationship between tunneling and dividend payments may vary depending on the country's institutional context.

## 1. Introduction

Related party transactions (RPTs) are well documented as a typical tunneling tool used by controlling shareholders to enrich themselves at the expense of minority shareholders due to their convenience in transferring benefits, lack of transparency, and complexity in evading legal scrutiny (Johnson et al., 2000; Jia et al., 2013; Kang et al., 2014). In emerging countries with weak safeguards for minority shareholders, such as China, tunneling through RPTs even impedes the long-term growth of capital markets (La Porta et al., 2000; Allen et al., 2024). In contrast to tunneling, a well-structured dividend policy signals fairness to minority shareholders and effectively mitigates agency costs (Gugler and Yurtoglu, 2003; Ghosh and Sun, 2014; Louis and Urcan, 2015).

The study re-evaluates the relationship between dividend payouts and RPT-based tunneling using the full sample of Chinese-listed firms from 2002 to 2022. As of the end of 2022, the number of listed companies on the Chinese domestic stock market reached 5067. The institutional framework remains underdeveloped, and there are clear deficiencies in the corporate governance of Chinese listed companies.

## 2. Hypotheses

- **Hypothesis 1:** Firms do not raise dividends to mitigate agency conflicts from RPT-based tunneling in China.
- **Hypothesis 2:** Firms with greater engagement in non-business RPTs pay fewer dividends.
- **Hypothesis 3:** Internal governance cannot effectively moderate the relationship between RPTs and dividend payouts in emerging markets.
- **Hypothesis 4a:** External monitoring negatively moderates the relationship between RPTs and dividend payouts in emerging markets.
- **Hypothesis 4b:** External monitoring cannot moderate the relationship between RPTs and dividend payouts in emerging markets.

## 3. Research Design

Data from China Stock Market and Accounting Research Database (CSMAR), A-share listed firms 2002–2022. Final sample: 4,852 firms, 35,108 firm-year observations. About 79% of observations have related party transactions. Variables include Dividends (dividend per share before tax / year-end share price), #RPTs (number of disclosed RPTs), and firm-level controls (FCF, MTB, firm size, ROA, leverage, tangible assets, CEO duality, independent directors, board size, firm age, cash flow volatility).

## 4. Empirical Results

### Main results (Table 2)

RPT count is negatively associated with both the likelihood and level of dividend payouts at 1% significance. A one standard deviation increase in #RPTs leads to a 4.57% decrease in dividend payments (17.27% of sample mean). Non-business RPTs drive the effect; business RPTs are insignificant.

### Non-tradable share reform (Table 3)

China's staggered non-tradable share reform (2005–2009) used as natural experiment. Parallel trend tests confirm no pre-reform correlation. Post-reform, the RPT×Post interaction is significantly negative, confirming that after the reform (when dividends became equitable distributions rather than tunneling tools), large shareholders reduced dividends when engaging in RPTs.

### Instrumental variable (Table 4)

Executives' concurrent positions used as IV. First stage: concurrent positions positively predict RPT frequency. Exclusion test: concurrent positions do not directly affect dividends. Second stage: instrumented RPTs significantly reduce dividends.

### Internal governance (Table 5)

CEO duality and board size do not significantly moderate the negative RPT–dividend relationship, consistent with ineffective internal governance in Chinese emerging markets.

### External monitoring (Table 6)

Institutional investor ownership and analyst coverage both significantly attenuate the negative RPT–dividend relationship, supporting Hypothesis 4a.

## 5. Additional Analysis

### Free cash flow mechanism (Table 8)

A one-standard-deviation increase in #RPTs leads to a 1.61% decrease in free cash flow to equity. Non-business RPTs drive this effect; business RPTs are insignificant.

### RPT inflow vs. outflow (Table 9)

Only outflow RPTs significantly reduce dividends. Inflow RPTs are insignificant, consistent with the free cash flow mechanism.

### Stakeholder awareness (Section 5.4)

The RPT–dividend relationship is significantly weakened in the year a firm is penalized by the CSRC for RPT violations and in the following year, suggesting that increased minority shareholder awareness of RPT damage leads firms to compensate with higher dividends.
