# Cross-border enforcement of securities laws and dividend payouts

- **Authors:** Chen, Xiaoqi; Hsieh, Chih-Chieh Chris; Tsang, Albert; Xiang, Yi
- **Journal:** The British Accounting Review
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.bar.2022.101117

## Abstract

Following the September 11, 2001 attacks, numerous countries signed a special nonbinding arrangement to enhance cross-border enforcement of securities laws. This agreement is the Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding Concerning Consultation and Cooperation and the Exchange of Information (MMoU). This study examines whether and how a country's MMoU entry affects firms' dividend payout policies. Relative to domestic US firms, foreign firms cross-listed in the US report a significant increase in dividend payouts after their home country entered the MMoU. Furthermore, the effect of MMoU on corporate dividend payouts is pronounced for (1) firms with high agency costs, (2) countries with low external governance quality, and (3) countries with poor disclosure environments. Furthermore, we find that MMoU significantly changes the corporate governance structure. Our findings collectively corroborate the prediction of the dividend outcome model and are consistent with the notion that strengthening cross-border regulatory enforcement of foreign firms improves protection for outside shareholders and increases dividend payouts.

## 1. Introduction

Conflicts of interest between corporate insiders (e.g., managers and controlling shareholders) and outside investors (e.g., minority shareholders) are central to analyzing modern corporations (Jensen & Meckling, 1976). Theoretically, insiders controlling corporate assets can use these assets to benefit themselves at the cost of outside investors' interests. Studies suggest that dividend policies address agency problems between corporate insiders and outside shareholders (Jensen, 1986; La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes, et al., 2000). This study examines how the extent of legal protection for outside investors, which traditionally mitigates agency problems, shapes firms' dividend payout practices. Specifically, we test whether a change in cross-border enforcement of security laws changes firms' dividend payouts.

MMoU serves as a solution to issues associated with increased cross-border market activities. After the September 11, 2001 attacks, regulatory authorities from several countries recognized the limitations of cross-border cooperation in the enforcement of securities laws. Hence, many securities regulators from countries worldwide have established a special non-binding arrangement (i.e., MMoU) to improve cross-border regulatory cooperation on information exchange and procedures for handling information requests.

## 2. Cross-border security laws, external governance, and dividend payout policies

La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes, et al. (2000) propose two models. In the outcome model, dividends are the outcome of an effective legal shareholder protection system. Outside shareholders use their legal powers to force companies to pay out cash, thereby preventing insiders from diverting assets. The substitute model suggests that dividends are a substitute for legal protection — firms pay out dividends to establish a good reputation to obtain favorable terms when raising external funds. Given contrasting predictions, we test the causal effect of cross-border enforcement on dividend payouts.

## 3. Hypotheses

H1: Foreign firms cross-listed in the US change their dividend payout policies after their home countries enter MMoU (direction based on outcome vs. substitute model).
H2a: The effect of MMoU is stronger for firms with higher agency costs.
H2b: The effect of MMoU is stronger for firms from countries with low external governance ranking.
H2c: The effect of the MMoU is stronger for firms from countries with poor disclosure environments.

## 4–5. Sample and Research Design

Final sample: 9,732 ADR firm-year observations and 9,732 matched domestic US firm-year observations from 2000 to 2018, across 48 countries. One-to-one propensity-score matching within same industry and year.

Dependent variable: DIV = cash dividends declared on common shares / total sales. Main specification: difference-in-differences with ADR×POST interaction (POST = 1 in year of MMoU entry and after).

## 6. Empirical Results

**Main results (Table 4):** ADR×POST coefficient is positive and significant (0.005, p < 0.01) after controlling for firm- and country-level characteristics. Cross-listed firms' dividend payout ratio increases by approximately 0.6% from a mean of 3% (a 20% increase). Consistent with the dividend outcome model.

**Country-level agency costs (Table 5):** ADR_HighAgencyCost×POST is significantly positive (0.008, p < 0.05) while ADR_LowAgencyCost×POST is insignificant. ADR firms in weak investor-protection countries pay more dividends after MMoU.

**Firm-level agency costs (Table 6):** ADR_HighAgencyCost×POST = 0.010 (p < 0.05), significantly larger than for low-agency-cost firms (p < 0.01 difference). Consistent across AC_FCF, AC_ER, AC_ATO, and CASH_HOLD proxies.

**Governance and disclosure (Table 7):** Increase in dividends is larger for firms in countries with poor external governance and poor disclosure environments.

**Robustness:** Dynamic DID confirms no significant pre-trend. Restricted sample windows (±5, ±3 years) yield consistent results. Seven alternative dividend payout measures all confirm. Placebo tests (randomized MMoU years) show near-zero mean coefficient. Controlling for IFRS adoption and board reform does not alter findings.

## 7. Conclusion

MMoU strengthens investor protection for cross-listed firms by facilitating cross-border regulatory cooperation. This leads to increased dividend payouts, particularly for firms with higher agency costs, from countries with weaker external governance and opaque disclosure environments. Results support the dividend outcome model over the substitute model.
