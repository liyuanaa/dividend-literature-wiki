# How do dividends signal when they are highly volatile?

- **Authors:** Cheng, Liubing; Chen, Yanyan; Liu, Feng
- **Journal:** Pacific-Basin Finance Journal
- **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.pacfin.2025.102794

## Abstract

This study investigates the signaling role of dividends in China, a market characterized by high dividend volatility. We show that dividend changes, especially increases, primarily signal short-term earnings information. Dividend changes generate only short-term market reactions, with no significant long-term price drift. Notably, the regulation from the China Securities Regulatory Commission diminishes the efficiency of dividend signaling. The information conveyed by dividend changes aligns with that from two other channels: Management Discussion and Analysis (MD&A) tone and investor platform inquiries. Companies strategically adjust the combination of dividends and MD&A tone based on future earnings to convey information. Collectively, these findings suggest that the efficiency of dividend signaling in the Chinese market is limited.

## 1. Introduction

Dividends represent a contractual cash return to shareholders. Lintner (1956) demonstrates that managers tend to maintain stable and gradually increasing dividends to meet investor preferences. This dividend stability is particularly evident among U.S.-listed companies. However, companies outside the United States typically exhibit greater dividend volatility.

Chinese listed companies distribute dividends annually with substantial variability. Between 2008 and 2022, 54.9% of companies increased dividends, 29.7% decreased them, and 15.4% maintained stable payouts, with an average adjustment speed of 0.82 — far exceeding the 0.14 average for U.S. listed companies. This raises a fundamental question: do dividends retain their signaling role when they become more volatile?

## 2. Institutional Background

In 1998–1999, over 50% of Chinese listed companies adopted a zero dividend policy. To address this, the CSRC introduced a series of regulations. In 2008, a key regulation mandated that firms seeking Seasoned Equity Offerings must maintain a cumulative dividend payout ratio of at least 30% over the preceding three years. This study examines dividend characteristics from 2008 to 2022.

## 3. Sample

18,874 dividend payouts from non-financial listed companies on Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchanges, 2008–2022. Financial sector excluded. Companies that do not pay cash dividends for two consecutive years or declare both stock and cash dividends simultaneously are excluded. Data from CSMAR.

## 4. Results: Dividend Changes and Future Earnings

**Short-term signaling (Table 3):** A 10% increase in dividends corresponds to a 0.03% increase in earnings change (ΔEt+1). Coefficient on ΔDt is 0.003 (t = 6.627) for year t+1, declining to 0.002 (t = 3.286) for t+2, and insignificant for t+3. Dividend decreases predict earnings declines for at least three years (I(ΔDt < 0) coefficients: −0.002, −0.004, −0.006 for years t+1, t+2, t+3 respectively), while dividend increases only predict one-year earnings improvements. Dividend increases even show a negative correlation with ΔEt+3 (−0.003, t = −2.781).

**Market reaction (Table 4):** Dividend increases are associated with positive short-term returns (Ret(−2,+2) coefficient = 0.004, t = 3.4); dividend decreases generate negative returns (−0.003, t = 2.399). However, neither increases nor decreases predict long-term buy-and-hold abnormal returns (BHARt+1), with no comparable long-term price drift found in Chinese markets.

**Stickiness of operating expenses:** The stickiness of operating expenses and R&D expenditures following dividend changes is identified as a key factor limiting dividends' ability to signal long-term earnings.

## 5. CSRC Regulation Effects (Table 6)

The CSRC's 30% minimum payout requirement (Targ30t) weakens dividend signaling in two ways:
1. Reduces the predictive power of dividend decreases for future earnings (coefficient for I(ΔDt < 0) in Targ30t=1 subsample is one-third of that in Targ30t=0 subsample).
2. Expected dividend changes (those required to meet the regulatory threshold) lack significant signaling value; only unexpected dividend changes maintain predictive power for future earnings (coefficient on Unexpected ΔDt = 0.004, p < 0.01 across three years; Expected ΔDt insignificant).

## 6. Dividend Changes and MD&A Tone (Table 7)

Significant positive correlation between dividend increases and proportion of positive words in MD&A (coefficient = 0.001, t = 2.987), representing a 0.056 standard deviation rise. Dividend decreases do not significantly reduce positive tone — reflecting managerial reluctance to explicitly communicate negative information. During earnings decline periods, only substantial dividend reductions (>50%) significantly decrease positive MD&A tone.

## 7. Investor Inquiries (Table 8)

Dividend increases raise the probability of dividend-related investor platform inquiries by 1.8%; dividend decreases generate 3.8× more inquiries (difference significant at the 1% level). Over one-year periods, dividend increases and decreases generate 5.2% and 10.7% more inquiries respectively (p < 0.01 difference).

## 8. Conclusion

Dividend changes in China primarily play a short-term signaling role. CSRC regulation both weakens the predictive power of dividend decreases and increases the costs of dividend-based information transmission, diminishing overall signaling efficiency. Firms strategically combine dividends and MD&A tone disclosures when conveying earnings information, preferring dividend reductions over negative MD&A language for adverse information, while using both channels for positive information.
