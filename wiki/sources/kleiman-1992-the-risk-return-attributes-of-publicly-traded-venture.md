## Abstract

Despite having provided original seed money to extremely successful start-ups such as Federal Express, Cray Research, and Apple Computer, the Small Business Administration (SBA) with encouragement from the U.S. Senate, has recently placed a moratorium on granting new small business investment company (SBIC) licenses. And, due to perceived fund mismanagement, at least some senators have suggested that the misguided SBICs could lead to "a mini S & L bailout." Consequently, the SBA has threatened the existence of the SBIC government-assisted program. Two public policy issues arise. First, should the government sponsor SBICs given their past risk-return characteristics? Second, if SBA support does re-emerge, should changes be made to the organizational structure of SBICs? With respect to the first issue, our study examines the risk/return attributes of two categories of venture capital funds: publicly traded business development corporations (BDCs) and SBICs. Popular folklore, as well as a recent study in this journal, suggest that a well-diversified fund of venture capital containing both SBICs and BDCs outperforms the general market. While previous research assumes that SBICs are similar to BDCs, we find striking dissimilarities between these groups. During 1980-1986, SBICs demonstrate significantly greater total and unsystematic risk, but significantly less systematic risk than BDCs. In addition, SBICs experience much higher returns, on a risk adjusted basis, than either the market proxy or BDCs. Financial theory suggests a number of reasons for the differential performance among the two groups. Moreover, given the strong investment success of publicly traded SBICs during the early to middle 1980s, and the ability of the public to monitor SBIC investment in lieu of the SBA, we conclude that continued SBA financing of SBICs benefits the investment community. However, the investment performance of SBICs in the late 1980s contrasts with the successes achieved earlier in the decade. Since 1986 many publicly traded SBICs have performed poorly, liquidated, or changed their organizational status. We conclude that many of these problems originate with the Investment Company Act of 1940. This Act limits operating flexibility of SBICs, discourages public trading and related monitoring by public investors, and reduces the equity capital cushion. Accordingly, we propose guidelines, including audit and equity capital considerations, that may mitigate difficulties stemming from the organizational structure of SBICs. Public policy issues related to SBA sponsorship of SBICs are relevant to individual and institutional investors-particularly as they relate to dividends, liquidity, investment allocation, and port-folio risk of venture capital related funds. For example, our study indicates that publicly traded SBICs, on average, appear to have greater corporate financial leverage and a large percentage of their portfolio invested in fixed income securities, compared with private SBICs (which invest more heavily in equity funds). To the extent that the SBA relaxes dividend payout restrictions on SBIC operating income, we might expect lower financial leverage and a greater portfolio allocation to equity instruments. This change would tend to reduce the financial risk related to SBIC ownership and encourage the provision of equity capital to emerging enterprises.

## 摘要（中文）

尽管小企业管理局（SBA）曾为诸如联邦快递、克雷研究公司和苹果电脑等极为成功的初创企业提供了最初的种子资金，但在美国参议院的敦促下，它近期暂停了发放新的小企业投资公司（SBIC）牌照。而且，由于被认为存在基金管理不善，至少一些参议员暗示这些误入歧途的SBIC可能引发"一场小型储贷危机式的救助"。因此，SBA威胁到了SBIC政府援助计划的存续。由此产生了两个公共政策问题。第一，鉴于SBIC过去的风险—收益特征，政府是否应当资助SBIC？第二，如果SBA的支持确实重新出现，是否应对SBIC的组织结构作出改变？就第一个问题而言，我们的研究考察了两类风险投资基金的风险/收益属性：公开交易的商业发展公司（BDC）和SBIC。流行的说法以及本刊近期的一项研究认为，一个同时包含SBIC和BDC的充分分散化的风险投资基金组合表现优于大盘。以往研究假定SBIC与BDC相似，而我们却发现这两组之间存在显著差异。在1980—1986年期间，SBIC表现出显著更高的总风险和非系统性风险，但其系统性风险显著低于BDC。此外，在风险调整的基础上，SBIC的收益远高于市场基准或BDC。金融理论提出了若干解释这两组之间业绩差异的原因。而且，鉴于公开交易的SBIC在20世纪80年代初至中期强劲的投资业绩，以及公众能够代替SBA监督SBIC投资的能力，我们得出结论：SBA继续为SBIC提供资金有利于投资界。然而，SBIC在80年代末的投资业绩与该十年早期取得的成功形成鲜明对比。自1986年以来，许多公开交易的SBIC表现不佳、被清算或改变了其组织形式。我们的结论是，这些问题中的许多都源于1940年的《投资公司法》。该法限制了SBIC的经营灵活性，抑制了公开交易及相关的公众投资者监督，并削减了权益资本缓冲。据此，我们提出了一些准则，包括审计和权益资本方面的考量，或可缓解源自SBIC组织结构的困难。与SBA资助SBIC有关的公共政策问题对个人和机构投资者都具有意义，尤其是就风险投资相关基金的股利、流动性、投资配置和组合风险而言。例如，我们的研究表明，与私人SBIC（其权益基金投资更为集中）相比，公开交易的SBIC平均而言似乎具有更高的公司财务杠杆，并且其组合中有很大比例投资于固定收益证券。如果SBA放宽对SBIC经营收入的股利派发限制，我们可以预期会出现更低的财务杠杆以及更高的股权工具组合配置。这一变化将倾向于降低与SBIC所有权相关的财务风险，并鼓励为新兴企业提供权益资本。
