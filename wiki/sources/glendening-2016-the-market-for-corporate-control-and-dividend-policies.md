## Abstract

This article examines the association between the market for corporate control and firm dividend policies. Specifically, we examine changes in firms' dividend payouts following a plausibly exogenous shock to the threat of takeover with the staggered initiation of country-level merger and acquisition (M&A) laws. Using a global sample of firms across 34 countries, we find that both the likelihood and the amount of dividends decrease significantly after the initiation of an M&A law in a country. Our cross-sectional analyses indicate that the negative effect of M&A laws on dividend payouts is amplified in countries where the institutional environment enables M&A laws to improve the takeover market and for firms that could readily use Interpol capital to finance growth opportunities. Moreover, this negative effect is attenuated for firms with already sufficient monitoring of managers. These findings suggest that the enactment of M&A laws, by strengthening the market for corporate control, lowered the need for firms to convey their commitment to shareholders' interests through costly dividend payments, especially when the threat of takeover prompted by M&A laws is likely to serve as an effective disciplinary mechanism.

## 摘要（中文）

本文考察公司控制权市场与企业股利政策之间的关联。具体而言，我们考察随着国家层面并购（M&A）法律的分阶段出台、企业股利支付在收购威胁受到看似外生冲击后的变化。使用涵盖34个国家企业的全球样本，我们发现，在一国出台并购法律后，派息的可能性和金额均显著下降。我们的横截面分析表明，并购法律对股利支付的负向效应在制度环境使并购法律能够改善收购市场的国家、以及能够便利地使用内部资本为增长机会提供资金的企业中被放大。此外，对于已经对经理人有充分监督的企业，这一负向效应有所减弱。这些发现表明，并购法律的颁布通过强化公司控制权市场，降低了企业通过代价高昂的股利支付来传达其对股东利益承诺的需要，尤其是当并购法律所引发的收购威胁很可能成为一种有效的约束机制时。
