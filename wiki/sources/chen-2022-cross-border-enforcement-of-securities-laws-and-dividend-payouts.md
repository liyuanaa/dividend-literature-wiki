# Cross-border enforcement of securities laws and dividend payouts (2022)

> **Paper claim** — Signing the MMoU (Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding on securities enforcement) increases dividend payouts for cross-listed firms, supporting the dividend outcome model: better cross-border legal enforcement reduces agency costs and compels insiders to disgorge more cash.

## Abstract

Following the September 11, 2001 attacks, numerous countries signed a special nonbinding arrangement to enhance cross-border enforcement of securities laws. This agreement is the Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding Concerning Consultation and Cooperation and the Exchange of Information (MMoU). This study examines whether and how a country's MMoU entry affects firms' dividend payout policies. Relative to domestic US firms, foreign firms cross-listed in the US report a significant increase in dividend payouts after their home country entered the MMoU. Furthermore, the effect of MMoU on corporate dividend payouts is pronounced for (1) firms with high agency costs, (2) countries with low external governance quality, and (3) countries with poor disclosure environments. The findings collectively corroborate the prediction of the dividend outcome model and are consistent with the notion that strengthening cross-border regulatory enforcement of foreign firms improves protection for outside shareholders and increases dividend payouts.

## Key Findings

- Using 9,732 ADR firm-years matched to 9,732 domestic US firm-years (2000–2018), the difference-in-differences estimate shows cross-listed firms' dividend payout ratio increases by approximately 0.6% from a mean of 3% (a 20% increase) after their home country signs MMoU (ADR×POST coefficient = 0.005, p < 0.01).
- The effect is concentrated among firms with higher agency costs: ADR_HighAgencyCost×POST = 0.008–0.010 (p < 0.05), significantly larger than the low-agency-cost group, consistent across both country-level (investor protection, earnings quality) and firm-level (free cash flow, asset turnover, cash holdings) proxies.
- MMoU's dividend-increasing effect is stronger for firms from countries with poor external governance (NATION_GOV) and poor disclosure environments (DISCLOSURE), with statistically significant differences between high- and low-regulation groups (p < 0.05).
- Dynamic DID confirms no pre-trend; placebo tests (randomized MMoU years) yield near-zero mean effects; results hold across seven alternative dividend payout measures and restricted sample windows of ±3 and ±5 years.
- Results support the dividend outcome model over the substitute model: improved cross-border enforcement acts as an effective legal shareholder protection system, compelling insiders to disgorge cash rather than reducing firms' need to signal.
