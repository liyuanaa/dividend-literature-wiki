## Abstract

The 2012 Dividend Tax Reform in China ties individual investors' dividend tax rates to the length of their shareholding period. We find that firms facing a reduction (increase) in their individual investors' dividend tax rates are more (less) likely to increase dividend payout. Such an effect is concentrated in firms where incentives of controlling shareholders and minority shareholders are aligned. Furthermore, investors respond to this tax law change by reducing trading activities before the cum-dividend day and successfully lower their dividend tax penalty. Overall, our evidence enhances the notion that individual investors' tax profiles shape firms' payout policies.

## 摘要（中文）

中国2012年的股利税改革将个人投资者的股利税率与其持股期限的长短挂钩。我们发现，其个人投资者股利税率面临下降（上升）的企业，更（更不）可能提高股利支付。这种效应集中于控股股东与中小股东利益一致的企业。此外，投资者对这一税法变化的反应是在除息日前减少交易活动，并成功降低其股利税负。总体而言，我们的证据强化了这样一种观点：个人投资者的税收状况塑造了企业的支付政策。

## Key Findings

- Using a difference-in-differences design around China's 2012 Dividend Tax Reform (which tied individual investors' dividend tax rates to holding period length), the coefficient on EVENT×HP (holding period proxy based on inverse share turnover) is positive and significant (0.045, t=2.48 in the full model); for two firms at the first and third share turnover quartiles, the difference in their dividend yield changes around the reform is 10.5% of the sample median yield.
- The tax-driven increase in dividends is concentrated in firms without divergence between controlling shareholders' voting and cash-flow rights; using related-party transactions (RPT) as a direct expropriation measure, the EVENT×HP coefficient is insignificant (−0.009) in high-RPT firms but positive and highly significant (0.089) in low-RPT firms, with a statistically significant Chow-test difference.
- Consistent with tax incentives mattering to investors, investors in high-dividend-yield firms significantly reduce their trading activities during the month before the cum-dividend day following the reform (coefficient on EVENT×YIELD_ANN is −0.048 for TURNOVER and −0.150 for ABTURNOVER, both significant).
- Ex-dividend day stock return analysis shows the coefficient on EVENT×TURNOVER×YIELD_CUM is positive and significant (15.747, t=3.18), indicating that after the reform investors who trade more before the cum-dividend day face higher dividend tax penalties — confirming they successfully manage their tax rates through trading behavior.
- Results are robust to a change-analysis specification, an alternative holding-period proxy based on changes in top-10 individual investors' ownership, and alternative payout measures.
