## Abstract

The economic value of the Say-On-Pay (SOP) provision of the Dodd-Frank Act has been a subject of debate. Proponents of this provision suggest these votes benefit shareholders by increasing investor influence over managerial compensation. Opponents of the SOP provision believe compensation contracting is better done by well-informed and unobstructed boards of directors. Our study provides direct evidence on the impact of the shareholder SOP votes by examining responses to the vote. We find that overcompensated managers with low SOP support tend to react by increasing dividends, decreasing leverage and increasing corporate investment. However, we find no evidence that management's response to the vote affects subsequent vote outcomes, nor do we find a subsequent change in firm value. Finally, we find excess compensation increases for managers that were substantially overpaid prior to the SOP vote, regardless of the outcome of the vote. Thus, it does not appear that the SOP legislation has had the intended effect of improving executive contracting. (C) 2014 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.

## 摘要（中文）

《多德-弗兰克法案》中"薪酬话语权"（SOP）条款的经济价值一直是争论的主题。该条款的支持者认为，这些投票通过增强投资者对管理者薪酬的影响力而使股东受益。SOP条款的反对者则认为，薪酬契约应由信息充分且不受阻碍的董事会来完成。我们的研究通过考察对投票的反应，为股东SOP投票的影响提供了直接证据。我们发现，SOP支持率低的过度受薪管理者往往通过增加股利、降低杠杆和增加企业投资来做出反应。然而，我们没有发现证据表明管理层对投票的反应会影响后续的投票结果，也没有发现企业价值随后发生变化。最后，我们发现，对于那些在SOP投票之前被大幅超额支付的管理者，其超额薪酬会增加，无论投票结果如何。因此，SOP立法似乎并未达到改善高管契约的预期效果。
