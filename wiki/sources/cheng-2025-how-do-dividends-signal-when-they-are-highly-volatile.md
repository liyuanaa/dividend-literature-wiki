# How do dividends signal when they are highly volatile? (2025)

> **Paper claim** — In China's high-volatility dividend environment, dividend changes primarily convey short-term rather than long-term earnings information; CSRC regulatory requirements diminish signaling efficiency; and dividend changes align informationally with MD&A tone and investor platform inquiries as complementary disclosure channels.

## Abstract

This study investigates the signaling role of dividends in China, a market characterized by high dividend volatility. We show that dividend changes, especially increases, primarily signal short-term earnings information. Dividend changes generate only short-term market reactions, with no significant long-term price drift. Notably, the regulation from the China Securities Regulatory Commission diminishes the efficiency of dividend signaling. The information conveyed by dividend changes aligns with that from two other channels: Management Discussion and Analysis (MD&A) tone and investor platform inquiries. Companies strategically adjust the combination of dividends and MD&A tone based on future earnings to convey information. Collectively, these findings suggest that the efficiency of dividend signaling in the Chinese market is limited.

## Key Findings

- Dividend changes predict short-term earnings (ΔDt coefficient = 0.003 for ΔEt+1, t = 6.63), but lose predictive power by year t+3 (insignificant). Dividend increases signal earnings improvements only for t+1, while dividend decreases predict earnings declines for at least three years — an asymmetry where the negative signal is roughly twice as strong and more persistent.
- No long-term price drift (BHARt+1) follows dividend changes, contrasting with U.S. evidence. Chinese firms exhibit average dividend adjustment speeds of 0.82 (versus 0.14 for U.S. firms), and this high volatility limits dividends' ability to signal long-term earnings, primarily because operating expenses and R&D expenditures are sticky following dividend changes.
- CSRC regulation (minimum 30% payout ratio for SEO eligibility, adopted 2008) weakens dividend signaling: the predictive power of dividend decreases for future earnings is one-third as large for firms already meeting the threshold (Targ30t=1) versus non-constrained firms; only unexpected dividend changes maintain significant predictive power across three years (Unexpected ΔDt coefficient = 0.004, p < 0.01), while expected dividend changes are insignificant.
- Dividend increases are positively correlated with MD&A positive tone (coefficient = 0.001, t = 2.99), while dividend decreases do not reduce positive tone — reflecting managerial asymmetry in communicating bad versus good news. During earnings growth periods, firms use both dividends and positive MD&A tone; during earnings decline, firms prefer dividend reductions over explicit negative MD&A language.
- Dividend decreases trigger 3.8× more investor platform inquiries within one month than dividend increases (p < 0.01); over one year, increases and decreases generate 5.2% and 10.7% more inquiries, respectively.
