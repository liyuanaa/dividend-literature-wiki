## Abstract

We use merger-induced changes to shareholder structure to test for active catering to dividend clienteles. Following mergers, acquirers adjust their dividend payout toward that of the target, but only when they inherit target shareholders through stock swaps. This adjustment is stronger when legacy shareholders are more influential and reveal a greater preference for dividends through portfolio holdings and trading behavior. Country-level differences in dividend taxes, governance quality, and population age further shape the extent of adjustment in ways consistent with dividend preferences. Pre-closing, differences in dividend payout discourage the use of stock as a payment method. (C) 2020 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.

## 摘要（中文）

我们利用并购引发的股东结构变化来检验对股利客户群的主动迎合。在并购后，收购方将其股利支付向目标公司调整，但仅当它们通过换股继承了目标公司股东时如此。当这些既有股东更具影响力、并通过持仓组合和交易行为显示出对股利更强的偏好时，这种调整更强。国家层面在股利税、治理质量和人口年龄方面的差异，进一步以与股利偏好相一致的方式塑造了调整的程度。在并购完成前，股利支付的差异会抑制以股票作为支付方式的使用。

## Key Findings

- In a global sample of 5,366 public-to-public M&A deals (1990–2015), acquirers in stock-swap deals where the target paid higher dividends increase their DPS by 14.23 percentage points more (of pre-merger BPS) in year 1 than in otherwise comparable pure cash deals — with the difference-in-differences (Panel B minus Panel C) significant at p < 0.01 across all three post-merger years.
- Multivariate regressions confirm that Dividend Gap alone has no significant effect, but the interaction Dividend Gap × Pct. Stock is positive and highly significant (0.276–0.453 across years 1–3, all p < 0.01), with zero effect in cash deals — isolating the catering mechanism to cases where target shareholders are actually inherited.
- The clientele effect is stronger when inherited shareholders are more influential (larger relative deal size, more illiquid acquirer stock) and when they reveal stronger dividend preferences through portfolio holdings (dedicated institutional investors, high portfolio dividend yield, tax-insensitive institutions).
- Country-level cross-sectional tests show adjustment is stronger when acquirer governance quality is weaker (relative to target), when dividends are tax-advantaged for acquirer investors, and when target domicile countries have a higher proportion of senior citizens — consistent with agency-based, tax-based, and age-based dividend demand mechanisms.
- Pre-merger, the probability of an all-stock offer is strongly negatively associated with the pre-deal Dividend Gap, showing that acquirers avoid stock payment when target shareholders prefer higher dividends — an ex-ante effect of clientele considerations on deal structure.
