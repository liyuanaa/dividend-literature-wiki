## Abstract

本文从行为金融学视角系统综述了股利迎合行为的主要研究成果和核心研究问题。梳理了投资者偏好异质性、公司规模和信息不对称对股利迎合行为的影响，以及回购政策和迎合行为确定的研究进展。从市场反应角度、未来公司收益及投资者对公司价值的影响等角度探讨了股利迎合行为对公司经营和利益相关者的经济后果。最后指出现有研究存在的不足，为将来研究者的后续研究提供了指导和启示。

*English summary:* This paper systematically reviews research on dividend catering behavior from a behavioral finance perspective. It organizes findings around: (1) determinants of catering — investor preference heterogeneity, firm size, information asymmetry, and share repurchases as a substitute; (2) economic consequences — market reactions to catering decisions, effects on future corporate earnings, and impacts on different investor groups including controlling vs. minority shareholders. The review identifies gaps and calls for future research on dynamic investor preference shifts, cross-country institutional comparisons, and AI/big data effects on preference identification.

## Key Findings

- Investor preference heterogeneity is the core driver of catering behavior; institutional and individual investors differ systematically in dividend demand, and firms adjust payout policy to match their dominant investor type.
- Larger firms face more external monitoring pressure, constraining their catering latitude; smaller firms exhibit stronger information asymmetry and thus greater managerial incentive and ability to cater to perceived investor preferences.
- Share repurchases serve as a flexible substitute for dividends in catering; managers choose between dividends and repurchases to maximize catering benefit, and repurchase timing and scale also reflect catering to investor preferences.
- Dividend initiations are associated with market premiums; cessation of dividends triggers significant stock price declines, confirming market sensitivity to catering-motivated payout changes.
- The effect of catering on future earnings is contested: maintaining high stock prices through catering can reduce financing costs and facilitate investment, but excessive short-term price focus may sacrifice long-term value creation.
- Catering that primarily serves controlling shareholders may harm outside investors; conversely, catering to minority shareholders can improve governance signals, stabilize stock prices, and protect investor rights.
- Future research priorities include: dynamic investor preference shifts and their effect on catering over market cycles; cross-institutional comparisons of catering mechanisms; AI/big data tools for real-time investor preference identification; and ESG-catering complementarity under sustainable development objectives.
