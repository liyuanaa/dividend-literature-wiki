## Abstract

Dividends are a key mechanism for shareholders to discipline managers and mitigate agency conflicts. This study examines whether the volatility of tax payments is associated with dividend payouts. Consistent with the predictions, results suggest that firms with more volatile tax payments are less likely to pay dividends overall and their dividends are lower in magnitude when doing so. These effects are economically significant and incremental to a firm's operating risk. The link between volatile tax payments and the likelihood of dividend payouts is weaker for firms that distribute dividends to alleviate agency conflicts. Similarly, the link between volatile tax payments and the amount of dividend payouts is weaker for firms that hold more cash for tax reasons. Taken together, these findings add to our understanding of the economic consequences of volatile tax payments and the determinants of dividend payouts.

## 摘要（中文）

股利是股东约束管理者并缓解代理冲突的关键机制。本研究考察了税款支付的波动性是否与股利支付相关。与预测相一致，结果表明，税款支付更为波动的企业总体上更不可能派发股利，且在派发时其股利在规模上更低。这些效应在经济上是显著的，且是在企业经营风险之外的增量效应。对于那些为缓解代理冲突而分配股利的企业，波动的税款支付与股利支付可能性之间的联系较弱。同样，对于那些出于税收原因持有更多现金的企业，波动的税款支付与股利支付金额之间的联系较弱。综合来看，这些发现增进了我们对波动税款支付的经济后果以及股利支付决定因素的理解。

## Key Findings

- Firms with more volatile tax payments (measured as the coefficient of variation of annual cash ETRs over five years, orthogonalized for operating risk) are 4.9 percentage points less likely to pay dividends per one standard deviation increase in tax payment volatility.
- Among dividend-paying firms, a one standard deviation increase in tax payment volatility is associated with 3% lower dividend levels, equivalent to approximately $6.8 million less for the average firm.
- The effect of volatile tax payments on payout probability is weaker for firms with high institutional ownership, consistent with agency motives (willingness to incur liquidity shortfall costs) moderating the link.
- The effect on dividend levels is weaker for firms with high unrecognized tax benefit (UTB) balances, consistent with precautionary cash holdings for tax purposes buffering against cash flow volatility.
- Firms with more volatile tax payments prefer more flexible payout channels (share repurchases, special dividends) over regular dividends.
