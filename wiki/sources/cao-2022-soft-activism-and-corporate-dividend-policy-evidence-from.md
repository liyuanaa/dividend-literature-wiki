## Abstract

We study the governance effect of institutional site visits and focus on corporate payout. Due to the weak investor protection and poor information quality in China, institutional investors resort to site visits as a form of soft activism. We show that site visits lead to an increase of corporate cash dividend, through the mechanism of disciplining management with exit threats. Such effect is more pronounced for visits by state-owned institutions. We further confirm the causal link with both difference-in-difference analysis and instrumental variable regression. The paper contributes to the research on soft activism and governance device of institutional investors in weak institutional environments.

## 摘要（中文）

我们研究机构实地调研的治理效应，并聚焦于企业支付。由于中国投资者保护薄弱、信息质量不佳，机构投资者诉诸实地调研作为一种柔性积极主义（soft activism）的形式。我们表明，实地调研通过以退出威胁约束管理层的机制，导致企业现金股利增加。这一效应对于国有机构的调研更为显著。我们进一步用双重差分分析和工具变量回归确认了这一因果联系。本文对薄弱制度环境下机构投资者的柔性积极主义和治理工具的研究做出了贡献。

## Key Findings

- Firms with institutional site visits increase cash dividend payout ratio (DPS/EPS) by 1.4% in the subsequent year compared to firms without visits; a one-standard-deviation increase in site visit intensity leads to a 4.1% increase in the standard deviation of the payout ratio.
- The causal link is confirmed via propensity score matching with difference-in-differences (using the first-visit year as event year) and instrumental variable regression (distance between firm's and institutional visitor's headquarter city).
- The mechanism is disciplining management through credible exit threats: visiting institutional investors significantly increase equity sales when the visited firm underpays dividends relative to peers.
- State-owned institutional investors have a stronger dividend-inducing effect than non-state-owned institutions, attributed to their longer investment horizon and greater power.
- The governance effect of site visits does not significantly differ between SOE and non-SOE target firms, suggesting exit threat is effective in both ownership contexts.
