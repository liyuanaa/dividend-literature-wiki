# Digital Finance and Dividend Policy: Evidence from China (2025)

> **Paper claim** — Regional digital finance development positively and causally affects corporate dividend payouts in China, operating through channels of reduced information asymmetry (which diminishes insiders' incentive to retain cash) and alleviated financing constraints.

## Abstract

This study investigates the effect of digital finance on dividend payouts. Using the digital finance index from PKU-DFICC and a sample of Chinese listed firms, we find a positive relation between digital finance and dividend payouts. Supporting our hypothesis that digital finance reduces information asymmetry and increases insiders' incentive to pay out dividends, the positive relation is stronger for firms with severe information asymmetry. Additionally, digital finance's role in reducing financing constraints also promotes dividend payouts. Overall, our findings suggest noteworthy implications of digital finance on financial markets via dividend payout policy.

## Key Findings

- Regional digital finance development (PKU-DFICC index) is positively and significantly associated with all three dividend measures across 18,381 firm-year observations for 2,507 Chinese listed firms (2011–2021); a one-SD increase in digital finance corresponds to a 14.8% increase in mean DVE and 27.5% increase in mean DVC.
- The effect is more pronounced for firms with greater information asymmetry — firms with low analyst coverage, non-SOE ownership, small size, and higher managerial ownership — consistent with digital finance reducing insiders' opacity-enabled incentive to withhold dividends.
- Digital finance reduces financing constraints (KZ index), and financing constraints in turn negatively predict dividends; this two-stage pattern is consistent with a financing-constraints mediation channel running from digital finance to payout policy.
- Causal identification via DID (exploiting the 2016 G20 Digital Inclusive Finance Senior Principles as a regional shock differentially affecting central/western China) and 2SLS-IV (mobile phone users per 100 people as an instrument) both confirm a positive causal effect on dividends.
- Of the three digital finance sub-dimensions, DF_Breadth (coverage) and DF_Depth (usage intensity) drive the effect; DF_Digital (digitization of inclusive finance for individuals/SMEs) is not significant, implying that the dividend channel operates through corporate-facing financial services rather than individual-facing inclusive finance.
