## Abstract

Quarterly earnings conference calls are becoming a more pervasive tool for corporate disclosure. However, the extent to which the market embeds information contained in the tone (i.e. sentiment) of conference call wording is unknown. Using computer aided content analysis, we examine the incremental informativeness of quarterly earnings conference calls and the corresponding market reaction. We find that conference call linguistic tone is a significant predictor of abnormal returns and trading volume. Furthermore, conference call tone dominates earnings surprises over the 60 trading days following the call. The question and answer portion of the call has incremental explanatory power for the post-earnings-announcement drift and this significance is primarily concentrated in firms that do not pay dividends, illustrating differences in investor behavior based on the level of cash flow uncertainty. Additionally, we find that a context specific linguistic dictionary is more powerful than a more widely used general dictionary (Harvard IV-4 Psychosocial). (C) 2011 Elsevier B.V. ## 摘要（中文）

季度盈利电话会议正日益成为一种更普遍的公司披露工具。然而，市场在多大程度上嵌入电话会议措辞的语气（即情绪）所包含的信息尚不清楚。使用计算机辅助的内容分析，我们考察季度盈利电话会议的增量信息含量以及相应的市场反应。我们发现电话会议的语言语气是异常收益和交易量的显著预测因子。此外，在电话会议后的60个交易日内，电话会议语气的作用超过了盈利意外。电话会议的问答部分对盈余公告后漂移（post-earnings-announcement drift）具有增量解释力，且这种显著性主要集中在不派发股利的企业中，这说明了基于现金流不确定性水平的投资者行为差异。此外，我们发现特定情境的语言词典比更广泛使用的通用词典（哈佛IV-4心理社会词典）更为有力。
