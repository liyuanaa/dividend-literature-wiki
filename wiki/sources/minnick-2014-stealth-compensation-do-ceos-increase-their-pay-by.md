## Abstract

Companies can increase executive compensation by allowing dividends to be paid on unvested restricted stocks grants, also known as stealth compensation. Examining all S&P 500 firms over the period 2003-2007, we find that more than half of the dividend paying firms allow this practice. We look at whether this form of compensation reduces agency costs or decreases value for shareholders. We find that CEOs' stealth compensation amounts to an average of $180,000 in additional income, which increases the CEOs' cash compensation and total compensation by 9% and 2% respectively. Firms engaging in stealth compensation have higher dividend payout ratios than those not allowing stealth compensation. For all firms using stealth compensation, there is a reduction in average ROA and Tobin's Q over the long run. However, stealth compensation companies with potential agency issues see a meaningful improvement in their long run performance. For weakly governed companies, stealth compensation may act as a bonding mechanism which may serve to reduce agency costs and therefore increase shareholder value. (C) 2014 Elsevier B.V. ## 摘要（中文）

公司可以通过允许对未归属的限制性股票授予支付股利来增加高管薪酬，这也被称为隐性薪酬。考察2003—2007年期间所有标普500企业，我们发现半数以上的派息企业允许这种做法。我们研究这种薪酬形式是减少了代理成本还是减损了股东价值。我们发现，CEO的隐性薪酬平均相当于18万美元的额外收入，使CEO的现金薪酬和总薪酬分别增加9%和2%。从事隐性薪酬的企业其股利支付率高于不允许隐性薪酬的企业。对于所有使用隐性薪酬的企业，从长期来看平均ROA和托宾Q有所下降。然而，存在潜在代理问题的隐性薪酬企业在长期业绩上有明显改善。对于治理薄弱的公司，隐性薪酬可能起到一种绑定机制的作用，从而有助于减少代理成本并因此提升股东价值。
