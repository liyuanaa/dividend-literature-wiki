# Social Trust and Dividend Payouts: Evidence from China (2022)

> **Paper claim** — Chinese firms in low-trust provinces pay significantly higher cash dividends than those in high-trust provinces, consistent with social trust substituting for formal governance mechanisms and reducing the pressure on firms to pay dividends as a governance device.

## Abstract

Social trust helps to reduce information asymmetry and agency conflicts between insiders and investors, and thus alleviates the pressure on firms to pay cash dividends. Consistent with this view, we find that Chinese firms in low-trust regions make higher dividend payouts than those in high-trust regions. The negative effect of trust is more pronounced in firms with lower information transparency and weaker corporate governance. We additionally find a lower market reaction to dividend announcements for firms in high-trust areas, and moreover that the role of social trust is more important for firms that are lacking in political connections and located in areas with weak institutions. Our results are robust to alternative measures of social trust and dividends and the use of the instrumental variable approach to alleviate endogeneity concerns.

## Key Findings

- Chinese firms in low-trust provinces pay significantly higher cash dividends than those in high-trust provinces (coefficient on Trust: −0.081*** for Dividend1, −0.090*** for Dividend2), consistent with social trust substituting for formal governance as an informal institution that reduces agency conflicts and information asymmetry.
- The negative effect of social trust on dividends is more pronounced for firms with lower information transparency and weaker corporate governance, indicating that trust operates as a governance substitute rather than a complement.
- IV estimation using historical number of wars in a province and number of distinct dialects as instruments confirms the causal relationship; results are also robust across seven alternative measures of social trust including blood donation rates and 1930s grain lending interest rates.
- Firms in high-trust regions experience significantly lower market reactions to dividend announcements, corroborating the view that the signaling function of dividends is attenuated when investors already have access to richer, more reliable information.
- The trust–dividend relationship is stronger in regions with weaker marketization and less effective legal systems, and for non-politically connected firms — consistent with social trust as an informal institution that substitutes for formal legal and political governance channels. This negative trust–dividend finding in China contrasts with positive trust–dividend findings in the U.S. (Hasan and Habib, 2020), together supporting La Porta et al.'s (2000) dual agency model of dividends operating differently under strong vs. weak formal institutions.
