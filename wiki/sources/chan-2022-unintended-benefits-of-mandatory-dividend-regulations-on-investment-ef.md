# Unintended Benefits of Mandatory Dividend Regulations on Investment Efficiency: Evidence from China (2022)

> **Paper claim** — China's 2006 mandatory dividend payout regulation produced an unintended benefit beyond investor protection: constraining free cash flows reduced overinvestment, particularly in SOEs, while the refinancing nexus amplified this effect for firms that sought public capital.

## Abstract

We investigate the unintended benefits of mandatory dividend payout regulations for Chinese listed firms seeking refinancing to enhance their investment efficiency, even though the initial intention of these regulations is to protect powerless external minority shareholders. We find that, while these regulations mitigate the overinvestment of all listed firms, this effect is more pronounced among refinancing firms than among non-refinancing firms. We further find evidence partially showing that the mitigating effect of these regulations on overinvestment is driven mainly by state-owned enterprises (SOEs). These findings suggest dividend payout regulations are used to partially ameliorate Chinese listed firms' investment inefficiency, particularly those of SOEs.

## Key Findings

- Since the 2006 CSRC regulation linking refinancing eligibility to cumulative dividend payout (minimum 20% of net profit over three years), cash dividend distributions mitigate overinvestment across all listed Chinese firms, and the effect is more pronounced for refinancing firms than non-refinancing firms.
- The overinvestment-mitigating effect is driven primarily by SOEs; the effect on non-SOEs is only minor, consistent with SOEs facing softer budget constraints and having greater propensity for empire-building.
- No significant effect of the regulation on underinvestment, suggesting the regulation is not excessively restrictive for firms with genuine investment needs.
- Similar results obtained for the 2008 regulation (minimum payout ratio raised to 30%, cash-only requirement).
- Identification strategy combines lagged dividends in regression models, propensity score matching with difference-in-differences, and three-way interaction tests around the regulatory change.
