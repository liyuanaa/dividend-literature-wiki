# Hot-Stove Effects: The Impact of CEO Past Corporate Experiences on Dividend Policy (2022)

> **Paper claim** — CEOs who experienced corporate distress events (bond rating downgrade, OCF shock, stock price shock, or dividend cut) in a non-CEO role at a prior firm subsequently implement more conservative payout policies: lower dividend propensity and levels, reduced repurchase activity, and a preference for repurchases over dividends — a behavioral "hot-stove effect" driving experience-driven conservatism in payout policy.

## Abstract

The personal traits of chief executive officers (CEOs) have been found to influence corporate policy decisions. We examine the impact of CEO past corporate distress experiences on payout policy. CEOs who have experienced a distress event in their career, while working in a non-CEO position at a different firm, subsequently alter corporate payout policy once in the CEO position. They are less likely to pay dividends and repurchase shares, pay out lower levels of dividends, and are less likely to increase dividends. They further exhibit preference toward repurchases. Overall, we report that experience-driven conservatism affects payout policy, a novel finding in the literature.

## Key Findings

- In a sample of ~4,228 US industrial firms (1983–2017, ~37,000 firm-year observations), CEOs with any prior corporate distress experience are 18.5% less likely to pay a dividend (DISTRESS_EXPERIENCES logit coefficient = −0.205, p < 0.01); the largest individual distress measure effect is OCF shocks (38.9% less likely).
- Conditional on paying dividends, distress-experienced CEOs pay lower levels: predicted dividend-to-assets ratio is approximately 12% below the mean for the composite distress measure and 42% below for OCF shocks, after controlling for standard determinants including size, profitability, growth, leverage, and lifecycle proxies.
- Distress-experienced CEOs are 7.5% less likely to repurchase shares and 12.8% less likely to use any form of payout; they are 11.7% less likely to increase dividends (p < 0.01), with weaker evidence of being more likely to cut dividends (p < 0.10).
- Conditional on making any payout, CEOs with distress experience are 12.1% more likely to prefer repurchases over dividends — substituting the flexible, non-committal repurchase for the sticky long-term cash outflow commitment of dividends.
- PSM and exogenous CEO turnover samples confirm main results (dividend propensity coefficients of −0.289 and −0.209, respectively). Effects are asymmetric: positive corporate experiences do not affect dividend policy, consistent with the pessimism bias that negative events have larger behavioral impact.
- Effects are stronger for more salient experiences: multiple distress events, top-5 executive roles, early-career experiences, and distress followed by bankruptcy all amplify the conservatism effect.
