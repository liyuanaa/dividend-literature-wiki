# The effects of a comply-or-explain dividend regulation in China (2018)

> **Paper claim** — The world's first comply-or-explain dividend regulation (Shanghai Stock Exchange 2013) successfully increased dividend payout ratios among Shanghai-listed firms, primarily through dividend increases rather than earnings manipulation, but complying firms subsequently issued more debt and experienced declining operating performance and firm valuation.

## Abstract

We examine the effects of the world's first comply-or-explain dividend regulation in China's Shanghai Stock Exchange, which requires firms to either pay at least 30% of profits as dividends or explain the use of funds. We find that many firms increased their payout ratio to comply, by increasing dividends or decreasing earnings. Firms with high profitability, state ownership, and fewer agency conflicts were more likely to comply. However, complying firms subsequently issued more debt and had a decline in accounting performance and firm valuation. The evidence suggests that the comply-or-explain regulation increased firms' dividends at substantial costs.

## Key Findings

- The 2013 Shanghai regulation was **effective**: the percentage of Shanghai firms with payout ratios ≥30% nearly doubled from 25.2% in 2011 to 54.6% in 2012, with no comparable change in Shenzhen (the control group), confirmed in difference-in-differences regressions.
- Complying firms primarily increased actual **cash dividends** rather than manipulating earnings downward, though some earnings management also contributed; dividend increase is roughly four times larger in magnitude in the treatment regressions.
- **State ownership and high profitability** are the strongest predictors of compliance; firms with higher management ownership or mutual fund ownership (lower agency costs) are also more likely to comply, while high insider-minority shareholder disparities discourage compliance.
- Complying firms are **more likely to issue debt** in the year following the regulation, consistent with the finding that US dividend payers often fund payouts with external debt (Farre-Mensa et al., 2018).
- Despite the payout increase, complying firms experienced **declining Tobin's Q and operating profitability** post-regulation, suggesting investors perceived the costs of forced compliance as outweighing the benefits, particularly because capital that might otherwise have been tunneled was replaced by expensive debt.
