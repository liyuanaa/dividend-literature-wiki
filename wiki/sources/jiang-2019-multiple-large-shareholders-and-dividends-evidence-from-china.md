# Multiple Large Shareholders and Dividends: Evidence from China (2019)

> **Paper claim** — Jiang et al. show that in the Chinese institutional context, multiple large shareholders cooperate to force firms to pay and increase cash dividends — rather than colluding to withhold them as in Faccio et al. (2001) — because after the mid-2000s crackdown on tunneling via related-party transactions, blockholders shifted to using dividends as the primary channel for extracting cash returns from their equity holdings.

## Abstract

Faccio et al. (2001) find that when East Asian firms have multiple large shareholders, they will collude to expropriate wealth and withhold dividend payouts. However, when it becomes difficult for large shareholders to expropriate wealth through activities like tunneling, large shareholders may then need dividends to fund personal cash flow needs. Thus, they may cooperate to make firms pay dividends and pay large dividends. To test this hypothesis, we study Chinese-listed firms. Consistent with our contention, we find that firms with multiple large shareholders are more likely to pay dividends and pay large dividends. These activities are especially prevalent after restrictions were placed on tunneling. We also find that dividend payouts and large payouts are more likely when the largest shareholder needs the cooperation of other blockholders to exert control over dividend payout policy, and when multiple blockholders are of the same identity.

## Key Findings

- Using a sample of 20,516 firm-years for 2,428 non-financial Chinese listed firms from 2000 to 2014, firms with multiple blockholders (Multi=1) show a 2.8% higher propensity to pay dividends and about 12% higher dividend-to-earnings ratios than single-blockholder firms.
- Multiple blockholders are positively associated with tunneling via related-party transactions in the first sub-period (2000–2007), but this association disappears in the second sub-period (2008–2014) after the crackdown. Correspondingly, the positive effect of multiple blockholders on dividends is significantly stronger in the post-tunneling era.
- Cooperation among blockholders to pay dividends is more likely when the largest shareholder needs the cooperation of other blockholders (i.e., when no single shareholder holds majority control), and when multiple blockholders share the same identity (private-to-private or state-to-state).
- Despite the negative coalition to force higher dividends, capital markets do not discount multiple-blockholder firms for these payouts, and the excess dividends are not associated with minority shareholder expropriation — firms with multiple blockholders pay more dividends especially when faced with fewer investment opportunities.
- Results are robust to 2SLS (using province-level average ownership structure as instrument), difference-in-differences around transitions in ownership structure, and propensity score matching.
