## Abstract

Big banks have become too complex for boards to govern them effectively, writes a former top executive at Bank of America and Citigroup. Directors need simple tools to cut through that complexity, and Krawchek suggests four: Pay top executives with bank debt instead of just stock and stock options, to give them more incentive to worry about risk. Pay dividends as a percentage of earnings instead of as a set amount, to preserve capital in downturns. Ignore net interest income in judging bank performance, and pay much more attention to customer satisfaction metrics. Focus board attention not on the most troubled business segments, as is customary now, but on those that are using the most capital. 146 Harvard Business Review June 2012 HBR Reprint R12060

## 摘要（中文）

大型银行已变得过于复杂，董事会无法有效治理它们，一位曾任美国银行和花旗集团高层的前高管如是写道。董事需要简单的工具来穿透这种复杂性，Krawchek提出了四点建议：以银行债务而非仅用股票和股票期权向高管支付薪酬，以使他们更有动力关注风险。将股利按盈利的百分比而非固定金额支付，以在经济下行时保存资本。在评判银行业绩时忽略净利息收入，而更多关注客户满意度指标。将董事会的注意力不放在最陷入困境的业务板块（如当前惯常做法），而放在那些占用资本最多的板块上。
