## Abstract

We examine how organizational form affects corporate payouts. Conglomerates pay out more than pure plays in both cash dividends and total payouts (cash dividends plus share repurchases). Furthermore, their payouts are more sensitive to cash flows compared to pure-play firms. The sensitivity of payouts to cash flow increases as the cross-segment correlation in a conglomerate decreases. Corporate payouts increase after mergers and acquisitions (M&As), especially among M&As in which acquirers and targets are less correlated. These results suggest that the coinsurance among different divisions of a conglomerate allows them to pay out more cash flow to their shareholders than pure-play firms.

## 摘要（中文）

我们考察组织形式如何影响企业支付。在现金股利和总支付（现金股利加股份回购）两方面，集团企业的支付都高于纯业务企业。此外，与纯业务企业相比，集团企业的支付对现金流更为敏感。随着集团内跨部门相关性的下降，支付对现金流的敏感性上升。在并购（M&A）之后，企业支付增加，尤其是在收购方与目标方相关性较低的并购中。这些结果表明，集团企业各部门之间的共同保险，使其能够比纯业务企业向股东支付更多的现金流。

## Key Findings

- Conglomerates pay out more than pure plays in both cash dividends and total payouts across all sample years (1981–2011): average cash payout of 1.40% vs. 0.87% for pure plays (p < 0.0001), and average total payout of 2.53% vs. 1.96% (p < 0.0001). The difference in cash-dividend-to-cash-flow sensitivity between a conglomerate with CORR_CF = 0 and a pure play (CORR_CF = 1) implies $0.834 more in cash dividends per $100 of extra cash flow.
- Panel regressions with firm and year fixed effects show negative and significant coefficients on CF×CORR_CF and CF×CORR_INV for both CASH_PAYOUT and TOTAL_PAYOUT, confirming that payout-to-cash-flow sensitivity is negatively related to cross-segment correlation within a firm.
- Falsification tests using pseudo conglomerates (portfolios of single-segment firms matched to each conglomerate segment by industry and size) show actual conglomerates have significantly higher cash-dividend-to-cash-flow sensitivities than pseudo conglomerates, ruling out industry and size explanations.
- Corporate payouts increase after stock swap M&As (dividend-to-cash-flow sensitivity rises by 0.47 percentage points); the increase is more pronounced when the cash-flow or investment correlation between acquirer and target before the M&A is lower, consistent with the coinsurance hypothesis.
- Conglomerates with more efficient internal capital markets (less diverse Tobin's Q across segments, per Rajan et al. 2000) have greater payout-to-cash-flow sensitivities than those with less efficient internal capital markets.
