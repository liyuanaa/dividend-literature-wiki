# 参股企业股利分配与上市公司权益资本成本 (2026)

> **Paper claim** — Higher cash dividend payouts by associates and joint ventures (AJVs) in which a listed firm holds a minority equity stake reduce the listed firm's cost of equity, by lowering downside risk in AJV investment income and by reducing information risk about the reliability of equity-method earnings.

## Abstract

本文以权益投资行为中的参股企业为切入点，探讨了参股企业投资股利分配对上市公司权益资本成本的影响。研究发现，参股企业股利水平越高，上市公司权益资本成本越低。机制分析表明，参股企业股利分配通过降低参股企业投资收益的下行风险以及提供投资收益匹配的股利信息以减少信息风险，是参股企业股利分配降低上市公司权益资本成本的两个重要机制。进一步研究发现，权益法核算投资收益占比越高、参股企业位于境外时，参股企业股利分配降低上市公司权益资本成本的效果越强。

*English summary:* This study examines how cash dividends from associates and joint ventures (AJVs) affect listed firms' cost of equity. We find that firms face a lower cost of equity when their AJVs distribute higher cash dividends. This relation operates through two channels: lower downside risk in AJV investment income and lower information risk regarding the reliability of that income. The negative association is more pronounced when AJV investment income accounts for a larger share of net profit and when AJVs are located abroad.

## Key Findings

- AJV dividend payout level (JAD) is negatively associated with listed firm cost of equity at 1% significance under both PEG (−0.0009) and MPEG (−0.0012) model estimates; a one-standard-deviation increase in JAD reduces cost of equity by ~1.07% of its mean.
- Mechanism test 1 (downside risk): the negative JAD–cost-of-equity association is concentrated in firms with high earnings volatility; when profitability is stable, the effect is insignificant—consistent with dividends reducing cash-flow uncertainty for investors.
- Mechanism test 2 (information risk): the effect is significant only when investor platforms have not yet covered the AJV; once covered, the additional information reduces the marginal value of dividend signals—consistent with AJV dividends substituting for direct information access.
- Heterogeneity: the effect is stronger when equity-method income comprises a higher fraction of consolidated net profit, and when AJVs are located overseas (where parent monitoring is weaker and information asymmetry is higher).
- Robustness is confirmed via PSM (propensity score matching), Heckman two-stage, entropy balancing, and instrumental variable approaches; results are consistent across alternative cost-of-equity proxies.
