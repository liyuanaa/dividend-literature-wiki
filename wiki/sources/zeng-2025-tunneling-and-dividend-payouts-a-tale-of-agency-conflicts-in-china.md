# Tunneling and dividend payouts: A tale of agency conflicts in China (2026)

> **Paper claim** — In China, RPT-based tunneling by controlling shareholders is negatively associated with dividend payout ratios, contrary to U.S. evidence, because minority shareholders lack awareness of the harm done by RPTs and controlling shareholders feel no need to signal fairness through dividends.

## Abstract

Related party transactions (RPTs) are often viewed as a means for majority shareholders to tunnel, while dividends ensure fair payout to both majority and minority shareholders. Using a sample of Chinese listed firms from 2002 to 2022, our study documents a negative relationship between RPT-based tunneling and the dividend payout ratio, a finding that is contrary to the existing literature on the relationship between the tunneling effect and dividend payout ratios in the U.S. market. In addition, our study finds that external monitoring, as opposed to internal governance, significantly limits the ability of large shareholders to reduce dividend payouts through tunneling. Further mechanism analysis shows that RPT tunneling reduces dividend payments by reducing available free cash flow. Minority shareholders' insufficient awareness of the damage of controlling shareholders' tunneling through related party transactions is an important reason for the existence of this negative correlation in China.

## Key Findings

- RPT frequency is negatively associated with both the likelihood and level of dividend payouts at 1% significance; a one-standard-deviation increase in #RPTs corresponds to a 17.27% reduction in dividends relative to the sample mean.
- Non-business RPTs (those involving directors, officers, or major shareholders as counterparties) drive the negative effect; business RPTs are insignificant, consistent with non-business RPTs serving as opportunistic tunneling vehicles.
- China's staggered non-tradable share reform (2005–2009), used as a natural experiment, confirms the causal channel: post-reform, when dividends became equitable distributions rather than additional tunneling tools, large shareholders engaged in RPTs further reduced dividend payouts.
- Internal governance instruments (CEO duality, board size) do not moderate the negative RPT–dividend relationship, consistent with the limited effectiveness of internal governance in Chinese emerging markets.
- External monitoring (institutional investor ownership and analyst coverage) significantly attenuates the negative association, supporting external monitoring as a functional substitute for weak internal governance.
- The mechanism operates through free cash flow: RPTs reduce FCFE by 1.61% per standard deviation increase in #RPTs, and only outflow-type RPTs reduce dividends, consistent with tunneling that depletes firm cash.
