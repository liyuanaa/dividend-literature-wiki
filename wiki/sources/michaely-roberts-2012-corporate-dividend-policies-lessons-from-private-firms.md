## Abstract

We compare the dividend policies of publicly and privately held firms in order to help identify the forces shaping corporate dividends, and shed light on the behavior of privately held companies. We show that private firms smooth dividends significantly less than their public counterparts, suggesting that the scrutiny of public capital markets plays a central role in the propensity of firms to smooth dividends over time. Public firms pay relatively higher dividends that tend to be more sensitive to changes in investment opportunities than otherwise similar private firms. Ultimately, ownership structure and incentives play key roles in shaping dividend policies. (JEL G35, G32, G15)

## Key Findings

- Public firms smooth dividends significantly more than private firms: the Lintner speed-of-adjustment parameter is 0.41 for public firms, 0.63 for private dispersed firms, and 0.83 for wholly owned firms; when firms transition from private to public, the rate of dividend omission decreases by 56% and dividend cuts decrease by 40%.
- Public firms pay relatively higher dividends than comparable private firms (27% of profits vs. 17.8% for private dispersed and 13.4% for wholly owned), and this holds both in matched samples and for the same firms before and after going public.
- Wholly owned firms' dividends are most sensitive to investment opportunities (sales growth), consistent with dividends acting as a residual payout when agency and information frictions are minimal; the same sensitivity is absent for private dispersed firms and weaker for public firms.
- A 1997 U.K. tax reform that reduced dividend value to pension fund investors by 20% did not change cross-group patterns, suggesting the observed differences are driven by ownership structure and capital market scrutiny rather than tax clienteles.
- Results are identified via both propensity-score matched samples and a transition sample (firms that underwent IPO or going-private), with both approaches yielding consistent conclusions about the role of public market listing in shaping smoothing behavior.
