# Foreign Institutional Investors and Dividend Policy: Evidence from China (2017)

> **Paper claim** — In China's QFII setting, foreign institutional investors self-select into high-dividend firms rather than actively promoting dividends post-investment; dividend changes predict subsequent foreign shareholding changes, but not vice versa, supporting the signaling over the monitoring interpretation.

## Abstract

This study examines whether foreign institutional investment influences firms' dividend policies. Using data from all domestically listed non-financial firms in China during the period of 2003–2013, we find that foreign shareholding influences dividend decisions and vice versa. Furthermore, changes in dividend payments over time positively affect subsequent changes in foreign shareholding, but the opposite is not true. Our study indicates that foreign institutional investors do not change firms' future dividend payments once they have made their investment choices in China. Moreover, they self-select into Chinese firms that pay high dividends. Our evidence suggests that in an institutional setting where foreign investors have tightly restricted access to local securities markets and a relatively high risk of expropriation by controlling shareholders exists, firms can use dividends to signal good investment opportunities to foreign investors.

## Key Findings

- Foreign institutional shareholding (QFII presence and proportion) is positively and significantly associated with both dividend levels and dividend-paying probability in cross-sectional Tobit and probit regressions.
- Simultaneous equation models (GMM) show foreign shareholding and dividends are jointly determined and mutually reinforcing: foreign shareholding promotes dividends, and higher dividends attract foreign investors.
- Change regressions reveal the direction of causation: changes in dividend payments significantly predict subsequent changes in foreign shareholding, while changes in foreign shareholding do not significantly predict subsequent dividend changes.
- Conclusion favors signaling over monitoring: QFIIs self-select into high-dividend firms due to restricted investment access and information disadvantages, rather than actively pressing incumbents to raise dividends after investing.
- Results are robust to alternative dividend measures (yield, payout ratio), alternative foreign ownership measures, and fixed-effect specifications.
