## Abstract

Using proprietary data on the entire spectrum of ownership structure and exact tax status of investors and firms, we examine how dividend taxation affects payout. Utilizing an exogenous shock to dividend taxation, we show that absent any frictions, dividend taxation has a large impact on payout. As agency issues and shareholder conflicts increase, owners' tax preferences have significantly smaller impact on payout. Three mechanisms reduce the dividend-tax sensitivity: Coordination among owners, heterogeneity in tax preferences, and diverging objectives between managers and owners. Altogether, taxation has a first-order impact on payout, but agency issues and shareholder conflicts mute its impact substantially. (JEL G30, G35, H24, H25)

## Key Findings

- In the absence of frictions (wholly owned Swedish closely held corporations), owners with tax preferences for dividends increased their dividend compensation ratio by approximately 7.3 percentage points (69% relative to pre-reform average) following Sweden's 2006 dividend tax cut.
- Tax sensitivity gradually declines as the number of owners (and thus agency issues and coordination costs) increases: the response falls by 40% when moving from one to three owners, and is statistically indistinguishable from zero in firms with five owners.
- Three mechanisms driving the muting effect are identified: (i) coordination costs among owners reducing substitution between dividends and wages; (ii) heterogeneity in tax preferences across owners making it harder to reach a consensus on payout policy; (iii) agency conflicts between managers and owners (separation of ownership and control) reducing dividend compensation responsiveness to taxation.
- Owners of closely held firms trade off dividend taxes and labor income taxes: they substitute dividends for wages when the dividend tax rate falls below the wage tax rate, but this substitution is impeded by ownership dispersion.
- The magnitude of effects is consistent across owner-level and firm-level analyses, and is robust to matched subsamples and fully interacted specifications.
