## Abstract

This study examines how financial reporting quality affects corporate dividend policy. We find that higher quality reporting is associated with higher dividends. This positive association is more pronounced among firms with more severe free cash flow problems and among firms with higher ownership by monitoring-type institutional investors. Further analysis of the relation between reporting quality and under-/over-payment of dividends suggests that reporting quality largely mitigates underpayment of dividends. Additionally, both a granger causality test and a difference-in-difference analysis of dividend changes around a quasi-exogenous reporting event yield evidence consistent with the direction of causality going from financial reporting to dividends. Overall, these findings are consistent with financial reporting quality acting as a governance mechanism that induces managers to pay dividends by disciplining free cash flow problems. Our findings support the view that dividends are the result of enhanced monitoring (Jensen 1986; La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes, Shleifer, and Vishny 2000).

## 摘要（中文）

本研究考察财务报告质量如何影响企业股利政策。我们发现，更高的报告质量与更高的股利相关。这一正相关关系在自由现金流问题更严重的企业中以及监督型机构投资者持股更高的企业中更为显著。对报告质量与股利支付不足/过度之间关系的进一步分析表明，报告质量在很大程度上缓解了股利支付不足。此外，格兰杰因果检验以及围绕一次准外生报告事件的股利变化双重差分分析，都得出了与因果方向从财务报告指向股利相一致的证据。总体而言，这些发现与财务报告质量充当一种治理机制、通过约束自由现金流问题促使管理者支付股利的观点相一致。我们的发现支持这样一种观点：股利是监督增强的结果（Jensen 1986；La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes, Shleifer, and Vishny 2000）。

## Key Findings

- In Tobit regressions on 19,031 U.S. firm-year observations (1994–2011), a one standard deviation increase in reporting quality (Dechow-Dichev measure) is associated with a 4.8 basis point increase in dividend yield — approximately a 5% increase from the sample average — and with both a higher probability of paying dividends and a larger magnitude conditional on paying.
- The positive relation between reporting quality and dividends is significantly stronger for firms with more severe free cash flow problems (above-median operating cash flows and below-median Tobin's Q) and for firms with higher ownership by monitoring-type institutional investors, supporting the outcome view of the free cash flow problem channel.
- The positive relation does not vary with financial constraints (Hadlock-Pierce index), providing no support for the financial constraint channel.
- Reporting quality is associated with lower underpayment of dividends (consistent with the outcome view) and also with lower overpayment of dividends (consistent with the substitute view or quiet life channel), based on tests using Richardson (2006) dividend residuals.
- Granger causality tests (PVAR) show that reporting quality Granger-causes dividends but not vice versa; a difference-in-difference analysis of firms subject to the SEC's 1955/1970 mandatory reporting frequency increases finds that these firms exhibit increases in dividends relative to control firms, consistent with a causal flow from reporting quality to dividends.
